Definition. Fix \(d\ge2\), a background \(\eta:\mathbb Z^d\to\mathbb Z\), and \(n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). Starting from \(\eta+n\delta_0\), a legal toppling at a site \(x\) of height at least \(2d\) subtracts \(2d\) grains at \(x\) and adds one grain at each \(x\pm e_i\). The configuration stabilizes if every site topples finitely often. The background \(\eta\) is explosive if \(\eta+m\delta_0\) is not stabilizable for some integer \(m\ge1\).

Theorem. Point-source regime. Let \(d\ge2\), let \(\eta_{\min}\in\mathbb Z\), and let \(\eta\) be a stationary ergodic integer-valued field satisfying \(\eta_{\min}\le\eta(x)\le2d-2\) almost surely. Let \(v_n\) be the odometer after adding \(n\delta_0\), and let \([x]\) denote a nearest point of \(\mathbb Z^d\) to \(x\). Almost surely, \(n^{-2/d}v_n([n^{1/d}x])\to\bar v+G\) locally uniformly on \(\mathbb R^d\setminus\{0\}\), where \(G\) is the Laplacian fundamental solution, \(\bar F_\eta\) is the deterministic effective operator associated with the law of \(\eta\), and \(\bar v=\inf\{v\in C(\mathbb R^d):v\ge-G,\ \bar F_\eta(D^2(v+G))\le0\}\). The differential inequality is interpreted in the viscosity sense. (Bou-Rabee, arXiv:1909.07849, Theorem 9.2)

Observation. In the displayed 4.2-million-grain finite sample, replacing the empty background by independent heights 0 and \(-1\), each with probability \(1/2\), changes the stable height pattern and the toppled region.

Theorem. Domain-background regime. Let \(d\ge2\), let \(W\subset\mathbb R^d\) be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and let \(\eta\) be a bounded, stationary, ergodic, integer-valued field satisfying \(\mathbf E\eta(0)>2d-1\). If \(v_n\) is the odometer obtained by putting \(\eta\) on \(nW\cap\mathbb Z^d\) and zero outside, then \(n^{-2}v_n([nx])\) converges almost surely and uniformly to \[\bar v=\min\{v\in C(\mathbb R^d):v\ge0,\ \bar F_\eta(D^2v)\le0\text{ in }W,\ \bar F_0(D^2v)\le0\text{ in }\mathbb R^d\}.\] Here \(\bar F_\eta\) and \(\bar F_0\) are the deterministic effective operators for the background laws of \(\eta\) and the zero field, respectively, and the inequalities are interpreted in the viscosity sense. The rescaled stable configurations \(\bar s_n(x):=s_n([nx])\) converge weak-* to a deterministic \(s\in L^\infty(\mathbb R^d)\), with \(s=\Delta\bar v+\mathbf E\eta(0)\) weakly in \(W\) and \(s=\Delta\bar v\) weakly outside \(W\). (Bou-Rabee, arXiv:1909.07849, Theorem 1.1)

Theorem. Explosive arrival times. Let \(d\ge2\), let \(p\in(0,1]\), and let \(\eta(x)\) be i.i.d., equal to \(2d-1\) with probability \(p\) and \(2d-2\) otherwise. Let \(M_\eta=\min\{m\ge1:\eta+m\delta_0\text{ is not stabilizable}\}\). Add \(M_\eta\delta_0\), use parallel toppling odometers \(v_t\), and set \(T_\eta(x)=\min\{t\ge1:v_t(x)>0\}\). Almost surely, \(n^{-1}T_\eta([nx])\to N_p(x)\) locally uniformly, where \(N_p\) is deterministic, continuous, convex, and one-homogeneous. Consequently \(t^{-1}\{v_t>0\}\) converges in symmetric-difference volume to the convex set \(\{N_p\le1\}\); Hausdorff convergence is not asserted. (Bou-Rabee, arXiv:2102.04422, Theorems 1.2–1.3)

Question. Identify the effective operator explicitly as a function of the background law. The theorem defines it implicitly through an ergodic limit.

Details

Proof idea, compact growth. The sandpile least-action principle is recast as a discrete nonlinear PDE. Subadditive limits of discrete subgradient measures identify \(\bar F_\eta\); compactness, a comparison principle, and viscosity-solution stability then identify every subsequential odometer limit.

Finite crossing lemma. In a box \(Q_k\), freeze the complement and one coordinate line at odometer one. If every parallel coordinate line contains a site of height \(2d-1\) and all other sites have height at least \(2d-2\), then a legal line-by-line toppling schedule makes every site of \(Q_k\) topple within \(k^d\) steps. For an i.i.d. Bernoulli background this cell event has probability tending to one as \(k\to\infty\).

Proof idea, explosion. A bootstrap-percolation comparison gives uniform linear crossing bounds. The parallel arrival time is compared with a last-wave process whose penultimate-cluster passage time is subadditive; the subadditive ergodic theorem produces \(N_p\), and deterministic wave comparisons transfer the limit back to the sandpile.

The same paper constructs random and deterministic explosive backgrounds for which \(t^{-1}\{v_t>0\}\) does not converge to a limit shape.

Growth and explosion in sandpiles were studied by A. Fey, L. Levine and Y. Peres, J. Stat. Phys. 138 (2010).

Definition. Fix \(d\ge2\) and \(N\ge1\). For integers \(k\ge0\), set \(\mathcal C_{2k+1}^{(d)}=\{x+\mathbf1:x\in\mathbb Z^d,\ |x_i|\le k\}\); for \(k\ge1\), set \(\mathcal C_{2k}^{(d)}=\{x-k\mathbf1:x\in\mathbb Z^d,\ 1\le x_i\le2k\}\). Put \(M=\lceil N/2\rceil\) and \(\mathcal S_M^{(d)}=\{M\ge x_1\ge\cdots\ge x_d\ge1\}\). Start every site of \(\mathcal C_N^{(d)}\) at height \(2d\), use a sink outside the box, and let \(v_t^{(d,N)}\) count topplings during the first \(t\) parallel updates. Then, for every \(t\ge1\) and \(x\in\mathcal S_M^{(d-1)}\), \(v_t^{(d,N)}(x,1)=v_t^{(d-1,N)}(x)\).

Theorem. Let \(d\ge2\), \(N\ge1\), \(M=\lceil N/2\rceil\), and \(\mathcal S_M^{(d)}=\{M\ge x_1\ge\cdots\ge x_d\ge1\}\). Start every site of \(\mathcal C_N^{(d)}\) at height \(2d\), use a sink outside the box, and let \(v_t^{(d,N)}\) count topplings during the first \(t\) parallel updates. Then, for every \(t\ge1\) and \(x\in\mathcal S_M^{(d-1)}\), \[v_t^{(d,N)}(x,1)=v_t^{(d-1,N)}(x).\] (Bou-Rabee, arXiv:2009.05968, Theorem 1.1(1))

Theorem. Let \(d\ge2\), \(N\ge1\), \(M=\lceil N/2\rceil\), and \(\mathcal S_M^{(d)}=\{M\ge x_1\ge\cdots\ge x_d\ge1\}\). For \(1\le j<M\), let \(\partial^\circ\mathcal C_{2j}^{(d)}=\{x\in\mathcal C_{2j}^{(d)}:\exists y\notin\mathcal C_{2j}^{(d)},\ |x-y|=1\}\) and define \(\tau_j=\min\{t\ge1:\min_{x\in\partial^\circ\mathcal C_{2j}^{(d)}}v_t^{(d,2j)}(x)\ge j\}\). Then, for every \(t\le\tau_j\) and \(x\in\mathcal S_M^{(d)}\) with \(x_i>M-j\) for every \(i\), \[v_t^{(d,N)}(x)=v_t^{(d,2j)}\bigl(x-(M-j)\mathbf1\bigr).\] Thus a translated corner of the smaller parallel-toppling process occurs exactly inside the larger one, round by round. (Bou-Rabee, arXiv:2009.05968, Theorem 1.1(2))

Question. For \(k\ge0\) and \(d>k+1\), prove the two identities above for the uniform initial configuration \(s_0\equiv2d+k\).

Details

Proof idea. The proof is a simultaneous induction on dimension, side length, and parallel-toppling time. The Babai–Gorodezky comparison method yields hypercube symmetry, a derivative-comparison estimate, and a parabolic least-action principle. For the initial height \(2d\), the resulting discrete derivative bounds are strong enough to persist under the parallel update; that regularity forces the central slice to obey the lower-dimensional recursion and also gives the translated-corner identity up to \(\tau_j\).

Equality of the odometers after every round implies equality of their increments. Thus the sites firing on the central plane in round \(t\) are exactly the sites firing in the lower-dimensional pile in round \(t\). The theorem concerns this time-resolved identity, not only the final shapes.

The displayed computation compares \(\mathcal C_{68}^{(3)}\) with \(\mathcal C_{68}^{(2)}\) through all 1,295 rounds, using one 34-by-34 positive-coordinate quadrant for each displayed slice. The cumulative site-round mismatch counts in that quadrant are 0 on the central plane, 21,149 one lattice step away, 35,066 two steps away, 60,403 three steps away, and 140,598 seven steps away.

The final height configurations are not equal: on the central plane, the three-dimensional heights exceed the two-dimensional heights by either 1 or 2. The exact identity is for odometers and firing sets.

Theorem. Let \(\varphi\) be a norm on \(\mathbb R^2\) whose unit ball has at least one boundary facet. Let \(U\subseteq\mathbb R^2\) be bounded, open, Pareto efficient, and compatible with \(\varphi\), and let \(f:U\to(0,\infty)\) be bounded and continuous. If \(u_n\) is the Pareto-hull peeling depth of a Poisson point process of intensity \(nf\), then, on an event of probability one, \(n^{-1/2}u_n\) converges uniformly on \(\overline U\) to the unique viscosity solution of \[\bar H_\varphi(Du)=f\quad\text{in }U,\qquad u=0\quad\text{on }\partial U.\] Let \(\varphi^*\) be the dual norm and \(\mathcal N^*=\{p:\varphi^*(p)=1,\ \#\partial\varphi^*(p)>1\}\). For each \(p\in\mathcal N^*\), choose unit vectors \(w_p,v_p\) with \(w_p\times v_p>0\) such that \(\operatorname{cone}(\partial\varphi^*(p))=\{cv_p-dw_p:c,d\ge0\}\). Then \[\bar H_\varphi(\xi)=\max\!\left\{0,\sup_{p\in\mathcal N^*}\frac{\langle\xi,v_p\rangle\langle\xi,w_p\rangle}{|v_p\times w_p|}\right\}.\] (Bou-Rabee–Morfe, arXiv:2110.06016, Theorem 1.1)

Details

Domain hypotheses. Pareto efficiency means \(\operatorname{int}(P(\overline U))=U\), where \(P\) is the Pareto-hull operator. If \(Q_p\) ranges over the cones generated by boundary facets of the unit ball, compatibility means that for every \(x_0\in\partial U\), some supporting convex cone \(\widetilde Q\) at \(x_0\) contains a cone \(Q_p\). Every bounded open Pareto-efficient set is compatible with every polyhedral norm; in particular, bounded open convex sets satisfy both hypotheses for polyhedral norms.

Proof idea. The supporting-cone characterization of the Pareto hull gives the discrete dynamic-programming principle. After an affine change of variables, each nondegenerate facet cone is governed locally by nondominated sorting. Half-relaxed limits give viscosity subsolutions and supersolutions of the effective Hamilton–Jacobi equation. Growth estimates prove \(\bar H_\varphi(Du)>0\) in the viscosity sense, and compatibility yields the boundary value \(u=0\).

For \(\ell^1\), the four facet cones are the coordinate quadrants and \(\bar H(p)=|p_1p_2|\). On \(U=(0,1)^2\) with \(f\equiv1\), \[u(x,y)=2\sqrt{\min(x,1-x)\min(y,1-y)},\] so \(\max_{\overline U}u=1\).

For \(\ell^\infty\), the facet cones are the coordinate quadrants rotated by 45 degrees and \(\bar H(p)=|p_1^2-p_2^2|/2\). On the unit square, \(u(x,y)=\sqrt2\min(x,1-x,y,1-y)\), with maximum \(\sqrt2/2\); its level sets are concentric squares.

Finite-sample measurements. No explicit PDE solution is used for the other displayed polygons. For three Poisson clouds of mean size 30,000 per polygon, the mean of the total layer count divided by the square root of the realized point count is 1.116 for the triangle, 0.952 for the diamond, 0.680 for the square, and 0.578, 0.428, 0.319, 0.255, and 0.215 for the regular 5-, 6-, 8-, 10-, and 12-gons.

For Poisson clouds of mean size \(2\times10^5\), the empirical mean over sample points of \(|u_n/\sqrt N-u|\), where \(N\) is the realized point count, is 0.015 for \(\ell^1\) and 0.006 for \(\ell^\infty\). If the unit ball is strictly convex, the Pareto hull equals the convex hull; convex-hull peeling has \(n^{2/3}\) scaling and an affine-curvature-flow limit.

Resolves a question of Jeff Calder; the strictly convex comparison is J. Calder and C. K. Smart, “The limit shape of convex hull peeling”, arXiv:1805.08278. Definition. Define \(\Delta_Fg(x)=\sum_{y:y\to x}g(y)-2g(x)\). Thus \(\Delta_F\) is the horizontal second difference when \(x_1+x_2\) is even and the vertical second difference when \(x_1+x_2\) is odd. A symmetric matrix \(A\) is integer superharmonic when there is an integer-valued \(g\) on the F-lattice such that \(\Delta_Fg\le0\) and \(g(x)=\tfrac12x^{\mathsf T}Ax+o(|x|^2)\).

Sections of the individual cones. M(a, b, c) is the symmetric matrix with diagonal entries (c+a)/2, (c−a)/2 and off-diagonal entry b/2. Recursion. The parity of a reduced fraction \(n/d\) is the parity of \(n+d\). For an odd parent \(p=n_p/d_p\) and an even parent \(q=n_q/d_q\), reduce both output fractions in \[C(p,q)=\left(\frac{n_p+n_q}{d_p+d_q},\frac{2n_p+n_q}{2d_p+d_q}\right).\] Select a node to compare the two parent tiles with the two child tiles.

Theorem. For directed edges \(E\) of the F-lattice, put \(\Delta_Fg(x)=\sum_{(y,x)\in E}(g(y)-g(x))\). Write \(A\preccurlyeq B\) when \(B-A\) is positive semidefinite, and let \(\Gamma_F\) be the set of \(A\in\mathbb S^2\) for which some \(g:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\) satisfies \(\Delta_Fg\le0\) and \(g(x)=\tfrac12x^{\mathsf T}Ax+o(|x|^2)\). Define \(M(a,b,c)=\tfrac12\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}c+a&b\\b&c-a\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) and \(\Lambda=\{(s-t,s+t):s,t\in\mathbb Z\}\). Then \[\Gamma_F=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\{A:A\preccurlyeq M(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,0)\}.\] In \((a,b,c)\)-coordinates its boundary is the graph \[c=-\operatorname{dist}((a,b),\Lambda).\] (Bou-Rabee, arXiv:2110.07556)

Question. Determine the integer-superharmonic sets for other periodic planar lattices.

Details

Write \((y,x)\in E\) for the directed edge \(y\to x\). In the paper’s convention, \((x\pm e_1,x)\in E\) when \(x_1+x_2\) is even and \((x\pm e_2,x)\in E\) otherwise, with \(\Delta g(x)=\sum_{(y,x)\in E}(g(y)-g(x))\). Thus \(\Delta\) sums over incoming edges: at an even site it uses horizontal neighbours, whereas the outgoing edges are vertical.

This convention was checked independently using \(h=n\delta_0+\Delta u\). In a 1,300,000-grain computation, the identity has zero residual at all 2,187,441 interior sites and the source odometer is 3,059,827. Interchanging the incoming and outgoing conventions produces nonzero residuals at 1,552,563 sites.

Proof idea. A Farey-type recursion constructs two recurrent odometers at every rational point of one boundary hyperbola; density and closure yield the remaining boundary. The label \(n/d\) corresponds to \(t=(d^2-n^2)/(d^2+2dn-n^2)\). The rotation \((n,d)\mapsto(d-n,d+n)\) pairs the displayed points 1/2 with 1/3 and 2/3 with 1/5, and paired odometers have period lattices of equal covolume.

Classifying the integer superharmonic matrices for other periodic planar lattices is open.

Divisible sandpile on a mated-CRT map

Definition. Fix \(\gamma\in(0,2)\) and \(\varepsilon>0\). Let \((L,R)\) be a two-sided planar Brownian motion normalized so that, for \(s,t\in\mathbb R\), \(\operatorname{Var}(L_t-L_s)=\operatorname{Var}(R_t-R_s)=|t-s|\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(L_t-L_s,R_t-R_s)=-\cos(\pi\gamma^2/4)|t-s|\). The \(\varepsilon\)-mated-CRT map has vertex set \(\varepsilon\mathbb Z\). Consecutive vertices are joined by one edge. For nonconsecutive \(a_1<a_2\), each coordinate \(Z\in\{L,R\}\) contributes an edge when \[\max\!\left\{\inf_{[a_1-\varepsilon,a_1]}Z,\inf_{[a_2-\varepsilon,a_2]}Z\right\}\le\inf_{[a_1,a_2-\varepsilon]}Z.\] Thus a nonconsecutive pair has two edges when both coordinates satisfy the condition. Start the divisible sandpile from \(t\varepsilon^{-1}\delta_0\); a vertex retains mass 1 and distributes its excess equally among its incident edges. If \(s_t^\varepsilon\) and \(v_t^\varepsilon\) are the stabilized mass and odometer, then \(D^\varepsilon(t\varepsilon^{-1})=\{s_t^\varepsilon>0\}=\operatorname{cl}\{v_t^\varepsilon>0\}\), where \(\operatorname{cl}S=S\cup\{v\notin S:v\sim w\text{ for some }w\in S\}\).

Toppling rule. On a locally finite multigraph, a vertex of mass \(m(v)>1\) keeps mass 1 and sends \((m(v)-1)/\deg(v)\) along each incident edge, where parallel edges are counted in \(\deg(v)\). Here \(m(v)=2.5\) and \(\deg(v)=6\), so each of the six edges carries 0.25. Total mass is conserved: \(2.5+6(0.50)=1+6(0.75)=5.5\).

Theorem. Fix \(\gamma\in(0,2)\). Let \(h\) be the field of a \(\gamma\)-quantum cone, let \(\mu_h\) be its LQG area measure, and let \(G^\varepsilon\) be the \(\varepsilon\)-mated-CRT map in its SLE/LQG embedding; write \(H_a^\varepsilon\) for the cell of \(a\in\varepsilon\mathbb Z\). Let \(\Lambda_t:=\Lambda_t(0)\) be the \(h\)-harmonic ball of \(\mu_h\)-mass \(t\) centered at 0. Proof idea. Quantitative homogenization classifies harmonic functions of at most linear growth by their asymptotic slopes. Ergodicity and local edge modifications show that a nonzero-slope corrector cannot be globally Lipschitz; a separate translation argument excludes integer-valued correctors with nonzero slope. For the finite-forcing theorem, Green-function asymptotics and planar open circuits through annuli show that infinite support implies \(\limsup_{R\to\infty}R\sup_{B_R\setminus B_{R/2}}|u|>0\), contradicting the hypothesis.

Proof mechanism

Circuits induce a random passage metric

1 · circuit routing

Between \(T(n)\) and \(T(n+1)\), each directed edge is traversed at most once. If \(T(n+1)<\infty\), the walk departs exactly \(\deg(x)\) times from every \(x\in A_n\). Boundary routing therefore defines a monotone map \(\Phi\) with

\[A_{n+1}=\Phi(A_n),\qquad A_n=\Phi^n(\{o\}).\]

2 · passage shape

The circuit passage time \[\tau(x,y)=\min\{n:y\in\Phi^n(\{x\})\}\] satisfies a triangle inequality and is stationary in law. The almost-live-path estimate rules out a zero time constant; the subadditive ergodic theorem then gives the deterministic convex limit \(n^{-1}A_n\to B\).

3 · circuit clock

Write \(d(S)=\sum_{x\in S}\deg(x)\). For every \(n\),

\[d(A_n)\le T(n+1)-T(n)\le d(A_{n+1}).\]

If \(|A_n|=\alpha n^2+o(n^2)\) and \(d(A_n)=\beta n^2+o(n^2)\), then

\[T(n)=\frac{\beta}{3}n^3+o(n^3).\]

This converts linear growth in circuit number into radius \(t^{1/3}\) and range \(t^{2/3}\).

Definition.
