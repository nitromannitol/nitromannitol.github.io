I

Random walks with memory

A random walk with memory is a walk whose next step depends on its own past, for example on which sites or edges it has already visited. After averaging over the orientations, the random walk on the randomly oriented Manhattan lattice is also a walk with memory: its first step along each horizontal or vertical line of \(\mathbb Z^2\) is in either direction with probability \(1/2\), independently of the past, and every later step along that line has the same direction. The basic questions are recurrence or transience, how far the walk travels in \(n\) steps, and the shape of the set of visited sites.

II

Abelian sandpile

The abelian sandpile is a model of self-organized criticality introduced by Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld. A vertex of a graph holding at least as many grains as its degree is unstable and topples, sending one grain along each of its edges. On a finite connected graph in which grains sent to a designated sink vertex are lost, every configuration stabilizes after finitely many topplings, and the final stable configuration does not depend on the order of topplings (Dhar). Adding a grain at a uniformly random vertex other than the sink and then stabilizing defines a Markov chain, whose stationary distribution is uniform on its recurrent states (Dhar). Two basic questions are how many topplings are caused by adding one grain in the stationary state, and the scaling limit of the stable configuration obtained from a large initial configuration, such as \(n\) grains at the origin of \(\mathbb Z^d\).

III

Quantitative homogenization

Homogenization describes the large-scale behavior of differential equations with random or periodic coefficients, and of the associated diffusions. For example, if the coefficient field \(\mathbf a\) on \(\mathbb R^d\) is symmetric, stationary, uniformly elliptic and has a finite range of dependence, then, for almost every realization of \(\mathbf a\), the diffusion with generator \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf a\nabla\) started at the origin, rescaled diffusively, converges in law to the Brownian motion with generator \(\nabla\cdot\bar{\mathbf a}\nabla\), where the deterministic constant matrix \(\bar{\mathbf a}\) is the homogenized matrix of \(\mathbf a\), also called the effective diffusivity. Quantitative homogenization gives rates of convergence in such limits. When the coefficients are unbounded and have long-range correlations, the associated diffusion can instead be superdiffusive, meaning that its mean squared displacement at time \(t\) grows faster than \(t\), or subdiffusive, meaning that the time it needs to travel a large distance \(r\) grows faster than \(r^2\).

01Brownian motion in a log-correlated potentialAt weak disorder and in every dimension \(d\ge2\), a Brownian particle whose drift is the gradient of a log-correlated potential has a unique scaling limit, whose path law is singular with respect to every mixture of Brownian motions.↗ 02Quantitative Einstein relationFor a reversible diffusion in a random environment under a small constant force \(\lambda e_1\), the asymptotic velocity divided by \(\lambda\) converges to \(\Sigma_X e_1\), where \(\Sigma_X\) is the effective diffusivity of the unforced diffusion; for almost every environment, the error is at most \(\lambda^{\beta^*}\) for all \(\lambda\) below a random threshold, with a deterministic exponent \(\beta^*(d)\in(0,1/4)\).↗ 03Critical long-range random conductance walkAt the critical exponent, for almost every realization of the conductances, the random walk converges to Brownian motion under the anomalous \(\sqrt{t\log t}\) scaling, with an effective diffusivity determined by the mean conductance alone.↗ 04Brownian motion in an incompressible random driftIn a critically-correlated incompressible random drift, a quenched invariance principle holds under the superdiffusive scaling \(\sqrt t\,(\log t)^{1/4}\); when the stream matrix has positive Hurst exponent \(\gamma\ll1\), for a typical realization of the environment the displacement variance at time \(t\) grows like \(t^{2/(2-\gamma)}\).↗ 05Random walk on orthogonal tilingsUnder mild deterministic regularity assumptions, random walks on orthogonal tilings of \(\mathbb R^d\), with a canonical choice of conductances, converge to Brownian motion modulo time parameterization. Examples include Delaunay triangulations with vertices sampled from a \(d\)-dimensional Gaussian multiplicative chaos measure.↗
IV

Internal DLA and divisible sandpile

Internal diffusion-limited aggregation (internal DLA) is a random growth model: particles start one at a time from a source, and each performs a random walk until it reaches an unoccupied site, where it stops. In the divisible sandpile, a site with mass greater than one keeps mass one and distributes the excess equally among its neighbors. On \(\mathbb Z^d\), with \(n\) particles or mass \(n\) started at the origin, the occupied sets of the two models, rescaled by \(n^{1/d}\), converge to the same Euclidean ball, almost surely for internal DLA. In the parking process, cars perform independent random walks and park at the first free parking spot they reach.

01Sharpness and critical scaling of parkingIf each site of \(\mathbb Z^d\) independently starts with one car with probability \(p\) and one parking spot otherwise, then at \(p=1/2\) the expected number of visits to a site in \(n\) rounds is of order \(n^{(4-d)/4}\) for \(d\le3\) and \(\log n\) for \(d\ge4\).↗ 02Quantitative explosion and percolation of the divisible sandpileFor every d ≥ 2, the set of sites that topple contains an infinite component at some mean below one.↗ 03Internal DLA on cylindersFor vertex-transitive base graphs \(V_N\) whose rescaled low spectrum converges and whose spectral gap vanishes, the average fluctuations of internal DLA on the cylinder \(V_N\times\mathbb Z\) are given by a Gaussian free field.↗ 04Divisible sandpiles via random walks in random sceneryOn every infinite, connected graph of bounded degree, the divisible sandpile with i.i.d. initial masses \(\sigma(v)\) of mean \(\mu\) almost surely explodes if \(\mu>1\), and almost surely stabilizes if \(\mu<1\) and \(\mathbf E[(\sigma(v)^+)^p]<\infty\) for some \(p>3\).↗ 05Internal DLA on mated-CRT mapsOn mated-CRT maps, a family of random planar maps which approximate Liouville quantum gravity (LQG) surfaces, the internal DLA cluster and the single-source divisible sandpile cluster converge in probability to LQG harmonic balls.↗

V

Discrete unique continuation

A harmonic function on \(\mathbb R^d\) that vanishes on a nonempty open set vanishes everywhere; this is the unique continuation property. Discrete unique continuation concerns analogues for discrete harmonic functions on graphs, such as: a discrete harmonic function that vanishes on all but a small fraction of a ball vanishes on a smaller ball. This statement holds on periodic planar graphs and fails on \(\mathbb Z^3\). Results of this kind are used to prove Liouville theorems and to study Anderson localization.

01Unique continuation on planar graphsA discrete harmonic function which is bounded on a large portion of a periodic planar graph is constant.↗ 02Rigidity of harmonic functions on the supercritical percolation clusterThe only Lipschitz harmonic functions on the supercritical percolation cluster are constant.↗
VI

Pareto peeling

Pareto hull peeling is a discrete algorithm, generalizing convex hull peeling, for sorting points in Euclidean space. It repeatedly takes the Pareto hull of the remaining points and removes the points on its boundary. The Pareto hull is defined from a norm; in the plane, when the unit ball of the norm is strictly convex, the Pareto hull is the convex hull.

01Pareto peelingWhen the unit ball of the norm defining the Pareto hull has a facet, Pareto peeling of a random point set in two dimensions has a scaling limit described by a first-order Hamilton–Jacobi equation, with an explicit Hamiltonian that is both non-coercive and non-convex.↗

Theorem. Record the line orientations by \(\omega\colon\mathbb Z^2\times\{1,2\}\to\{-1,1\}\): the line through \(z\) parallel to \(e_i\) points along \(\omega(z,i)e_i\). Call \(\omega\) the environment, and write \(p_n^\omega(z,z')\) for the probability that the walk started at \(z\) in the environment \(\omega\) is at \(z'\) after \(n\) steps. For almost every environment \(\omega\), \[\sum_{n\geq0}p_n^\omega(x,x)<\infty\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathbb Z^2.\] That is, the walk is transient almost surely. (Bou-Rabee–Peres, Theorem 1.1)

Deterministic orientations

One fair coin directs each line, so every edge of a line points the same way. Transience does not hold for every deterministic environment: for the alternating orientations \(\omega(z,1)=(-1)^{z_2}\) and \(\omega(z,2)=(-1)^{z_1}\), the walk can be mapped to simple random walk on \(\mathbb Z^2\), as detailed by Beaton and Holmes (2023), who report this as an observation of Guillotin-Plantard.

Once-reinforced random walk

Definition. In once-reinforced random walk on \(\mathbb Z^d\), every edge has weight one until the walk first crosses it and weight \(\beta\ge1\) from then on. At each step the walker moves to one of the \(2d\) neighbours with probability proportional to the weight of the edge it crosses. For \(\beta>1\), the walk favours previously crossed edges. Let \((X_n)_{n\ge0}\) be the walk started at the origin, and write \(\mathbb P_\beta\) and \(\mathbb E_\beta\) for its law and expectation. The range at time \(n\) is \(R_n=\{X_0,\ldots,X_n\}\).

Theorem (Range lower bound). For every \(d\ge2\), there is \(c(d)>0\) such that, for all \(\beta\ge1\) and \(n\ge0\), \[\mathbb E_\beta |R_n|\ge c(d)\left(\frac n\beta\right)^{d/(d+1)}.\] In two dimensions the exponent is \(2/3\). (Bou-Rabee–Peres, Theorem 1.1)

Proof overview

Let \(\sigma_k\) be the first time at which the walk has crossed \(k\) distinct edges. The proof shows that \(\mathbb E_\beta\sigma_k\le C(d)\beta k^{1+1/d}\); Markov’s inequality then gives the range lower bound by taking \(k\) of order \((n/\beta)^{d/(d+1)}\). The proof sums exit times of simple random walk over increasing sets, and uses a supermartingale built from the expected time for simple random walk to leave the set of sites whose incident edges have all been crossed. It requires no assumption on the shape of the visited set or on mixing inside it.

The paper also proves a stretched-exponential tail: there is \(C(d)\ge1\) such that, for every \(\beta\ge1\) and every \(n\) with \(n/\beta\ge C(d)\), \(\mathbb P_\beta\{|R_n|<C(d)^{-1}(n/\beta)^{d/(d+1)}\}\le\exp(-C(d)^{-1}(n/\beta)^{(d-1)/(d+1)})\). The Borel–Cantelli lemma then gives \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}|R_n|n^{-d/(d+1)}\ge C(d)^{-1}\beta^{-d/(d+1)}\) almost surely.

Question. Characterize \(\bar F_\eta\), which the first theorem defines only implicitly, in terms of the law of \(\eta\).

Details

Proof idea, first theorem. The sandpile is expressed as the solution to a nonlinear discrete PDE, and the proof follows the stochastic homogenization program of Armstrong and Smart (2014) in three steps: identify the effective equation \(\bar F_\eta\) via the subadditive ergodic theorem applied to an auxiliary Monge–Ampère measure; show that \(\bar F_\eta\) inherits the comparison principle for sandpiles; and show that every subsequential limit of the rescaled odometers is a viscosity solution.

Crossing time. Let \(Q_k=\{x\in\mathbb Z^d:1\le x_i\le k\}\) and let \(L\) be a line crossing \(Q_k\) in a coordinate direction. Run parallel toppling with the odometer frozen at 0 outside \(Q_k\) and at 1 on \(L\). If every line in \(Q_k\) parallel to \(L\) contains at least one site with \(2d-1\) grains, then every site of \(Q_k\) topples within \(k^d\) steps. Under the product measure, this event has probability at least \(1-k^{d-1}e^{-2\epsilon^2k}\) for every \(0<\epsilon<p\).

Proof idea, third theorem. A high-probability bound on the crossing time of a large cube gives a coarsened lattice with an infinite cluster of good cubes; with large-deviation bounds for the chemical distance of supercritical Bernoulli percolation, this gives uniform linear bounds on the arrival times. The arrival times are not subadditive, so the proof passes to the last-wave, an exploding sandpile in which the origin is constrained to topple a fixed number of times. The last-wave is approximated by a quantity which is exactly subadditive, and a deterministic comparison, which requires \(\eta\ge2d-2\), shows that the last-wave approximates the expanding front of the exploding sandpile.

For each \(d\ge2\), arXiv:2102.04422 (Theorem 6.1) also constructs explosive backgrounds \(\eta\not\ge2d-2\) on \(\mathbb Z^d\), random or periodic, which fail to have a limit shape: with \(T(n)=\min\{t>0:v_t(ne_1)>0\}\), \(\limsup_{n}n^{-1}T(n)\ge3/2\) and \(\liminf_{n}n^{-1}T(n)=1\).

Sandpile avalanches in two dimensions

Definition. A sandpile on \(\mathbb Z^2\) assigns a number of grains to each site. A site with at least four grains topples, sending one grain to each of its four neighbors, and a sandpile is stable if every site has at most three grains. A box \([-N,N]^2\) with wired boundary conditions is the graph obtained from \(\mathbb Z^2\) by identifying all sites outside the box to a single vertex, the sink, and deleting the loops at the sink; grains sent to the sink disappear. In this box, adding grains at uniformly chosen sites and toppling until the sandpile is stable defines a Markov chain whose stationary distribution is uniform on the recurrent sandpiles: the stable sandpiles that can be obtained from every sandpile by a combination of adding grains and selectively toppling unstable sites. The stationary state \(\mu\) on \(\mathbb Z^2\) is the local limit of these uniform measures as the box grows. The topplings that follow the addition of a grain to a stable sandpile form an avalanche. For a sandpile \(\eta\), let \(\eta+\delta_0\) be the sandpile obtained by adding one grain at the origin; if a finite sequence of topplings takes \(\eta+\delta_0\) to a stable sandpile, then \(\eta+\delta_0\) stabilizes. The radius \(\rho\) of the avalanche is the largest \(\ell^\infty\) distance from the origin of a site that topples, with \(\rho=0\) when no site topples and \(\rho=\infty\) when \(\eta+\delta_0\) does not stabilize.

In two dimensions the origin topples infinitely many times in expectation, and the expected radius of an avalanche is infinite (Bhupatiraju, Hanson and Járai). Nevertheless, avalanches on \(\mathbb Z^2\) are almost surely finite.

Theorem. For \(\mu\)-almost every sandpile \(\eta\), the sandpile \(\eta+\delta_0\) stabilizes. Moreover, there are constants \(a>0\) and \(C<\infty\) such that every \(M\geq0\) satisfies \[\mu(\rho>M)\leq C(\log(M+2))^{-a}.\] (Bou-Rabee, Theorem 1.1)

Proof idea. While the origin is unstable, topple it once and then stabilize the other sites, treating the origin as a sink; the sites that topple form a wave. Apart from rare cases, a large avalanche contains a wave that contains every marked site followed immediately by one that misses a marked site. Applying the burning bijection separately to a wave and to the rest of the box bounds the expected number of such pairs by \(\mathbb E[1/\mathscr L]\) for a uniform spanning tree of the box. At each of order \(\log\log r\) scales \(s\), effective-conductance and variance bounds show that, given the earlier scales, \(\mathscr L\) is at least a constant times \(\log s\) with probability at least a universal \(\varepsilon>0\), unless an earlier branch of the tree wanders far; hence \(\mathbb E[1/\mathscr L]\le C(\log r)^{-a}\).

Details

Domain hypotheses. The set \(U\) is Pareto efficient if \(\operatorname{int}(P(\overline U))=U\), where \(P(\overline U)\) is the Pareto hull of \(\overline U\). A convex cone \(Q\) supports \(U\) at \(x_0\in\partial U\) if \((x_0+Q)\cap\overline U\subseteq\partial U\). For \(p\in\mathcal N^*\), the flat cone is \(Q_p=\{aw_p+bv_p:a,b\ge0\}\). The set \(U\) is compatible with \(\varphi\) if, for each \(x_0\in\partial U\), some convex cone that supports \(U\) at \(x_0\) contains \(Q_p\) for some \(p\in\mathcal N^*\). A norm is polyhedral if its unit ball is a polygon. Every bounded, open, Pareto efficient set is compatible with every polyhedral norm, and convex sets are Pareto efficient.

Proof idea. A characterization of Pareto hulls by the flat cones \(Q_p\), due to Durier and Michelot (1986), shows that the height function is determined by a dynamic programming principle. Locally near a point where \(\bar H_\varphi(D\bar u)>0\), an affine transformation of nondominated sorting describes the behaviour of the height function. The upper and lower half-relaxed limits are a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution of the limiting equation. A series of growth lemmas shows that \(\bar H_\varphi(D\bar u)>0\) holds in the viscosity sense, and compatibility ensures that \(\bar u=0\) on \(\partial U\).

For \(\ell^1\), the cones determined by the four facets of the unit ball are the coordinate quadrants and \(\bar H_\varphi(\xi)=|\xi_1\xi_2|\). On \(U=(0,1)^2\) with \(f\equiv1\), \[u(x,y)=2\sqrt{\min(x,1-x)\min(y,1-y)},\] so \(\max_{\overline U}u=1\).

For \(\ell^\infty\), the cones determined by the facets of the unit ball are the coordinate quadrants rotated by 45 degrees and \(\bar H_\varphi(\xi)=|\xi_1^2-\xi_2^2|/2\). On the unit square, \(u(x,y)=\sqrt2\min(x,1-x,y,1-y)\), with maximum \(\sqrt2/2\); its level sets are concentric squares.

Proof idea. Put \(\zeta(v)=(\sigma(v)-1)/\deg(v)\), let \(X\) be simple random walk, and write \(S_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta(X_k)\). With \([a]_+=\max\{a,0\}\), and the supremum over stopping times \(\tau\) of \(X\) bounded by \(n\), the parallel odometer satisfies

Thus stabilization is equivalent to finiteness of an optimal-stopping value for random walk in random scenery. The moment condition is nearly sharp: for every \(p<3\), the paper constructs bounded-degree graphs on which, for every \(\mu<1\), there exist i.i.d. masses bounded from below with mean \(\mu\) and finite \(p\)-th moment that explode almost surely. Details

Proof idea. By the large-scale regularity theory of Armstrong and Dario (2018), every harmonic function of at most linear growth on the cluster can be written uniquely as \(a+\xi\cdot x+\chi_\xi(x)\), where \(\chi_\xi\) is the corrector; \(\ell_\xi=\xi\cdot x+\chi_\xi\) is the corrected plane. For the first theorem, ergodicity reduces to the slope \(e_1\), and a modification of the environment involving a finite number of edges forces \(\ell_{e_1}\) to have an arbitrarily large Lipschitz constant. For the second theorem, the gadget below forces an integer-valued corrected plane to oscillate exponentially, which the bounds on the corrector exclude. For the third theorem, homogenization of the Green function gives the leading term \(\sum_i\big(\sum_y f(y)\ell_{e_i}(y)\big)\partial_i\bar G\) of \(u\), which decays like \(|x|^{-1}\); when its coefficients vanish, a martingale sensitivity argument and topological obstructions in the level sets of \(u\), which use planarity, show that \(u\) has finite support.

The gadget. The effective resistance of \(T_n\) satisfies \(R_1=3\) and \(R_{n+1}=(3R_n+2)/(R_n+1)\). In lowest terms \(R_n=A_{n+1}/B_n\), where \(A_0=A_1=1\), \(B_0=0\), \(B_1=1\), and \(A_m=4A_{m-1}-A_{m-2}\), \(B_m=4B_{m-1}-B_{m-2}\) for \(m\ge2\); moreover \(A_{n+1}>3^{n-1}\). Harmonicity gives \(u(a)-u(s)=B_n(u(t)-u(s))/A_{n+1}\), so if \(u\) is integer-valued and \(u(t)\ne u(s)\), then \(A_{n+1}\) divides \(u(t)-u(s)\) and \(|u(t)-u(s)|>3^{n-1}\). By finite energy, for every large \(r\), with probability at least \(c\exp(-Cr^d)\) the cluster in a cube of side \(r\) contains a copy of \(T_r\) on which \(\ell_\xi\) is not constant; an integer-valued \(\ell_\xi\) would then oscillate by at least \(c3^r\) there, which for \(r\) of order \(\log N\) contradicts the stretched-exponential bound on the probability that the corrector oscillates by \(N^2\) on a cube of side \(N\).

Each coordinate of the harmonic embedding of the infinite cluster is a corrected plane \(\ell_{e_i}\), which differs from \(x_i\) by a sublinear corrector; by the first theorem, no coordinate is Lipschitz.

If a vertex has degree one, harmonicity forces its value to equal its neighbour’s value. Consequently, a finite tree attached to the rest of the cluster at one vertex is mapped by the harmonic embedding to the image of that vertex. Benjamini, Duminil-Copin, Kozma and Yadin (2015) showed that the space of linearly growing harmonic functions on the cluster has the same dimension as that of \(\mathbb R^d\), and asked whether there are nontrivial Lipschitz harmonic functions on the cluster; the first theorem answers this question. The proofs rely on the large-scale regularity of harmonic functions on the cluster established by Armstrong and Dario (2018).

Definition. Fix a nonnegative radial mollifier \(\rho\in C_c^\infty(B_1)\) with \(\int_{\mathbb R^2}\rho=1\). Let \(\Phi\) be a standard Gaussian free field on \(\mathbb R^2\), the Gaussian field with covariance kernel \(-\frac1{2\pi}\log|x-y|\) acting on mean-zero test functions, set \(\psi=\rho*\Phi\), and let \(f=\nabla^\perp\psi=(\partial_2\psi,-\partial_1\psi)\). For a molecular diffusivity \(\nu>0\), the diffusion solves \(dX_t=f(X_t)\,dt+\sqrt{2\nu}\,dW_t\) with \(X_0=0\), where \(W\) is standard two-dimensional Brownian motion. Since \(f\cdot\nabla\psi=0\), the drift is tangent to the level sets of \(\psi\).

Quenched superdiffusive invariance principle

\(\mathbf E_0^f\) denotes the expectation for the process started at \(0\), conditioned on \(f\), and \(M_f(t)=\mathbf E_0^f|X_t|^2\).

Driving Brownian motion
\((\mathbf E|\sqrt{2\nu}W_t|^2)^{1/2}=2\sqrt{\nu t}\)

\(M_f(t)^{1/2}\sim\sqrt2\,\pi^{-1/4}\,\sqrt t(\log t)^{1/4}\) in probability

\(|\log\varepsilon^2|^{-1/4}\varepsilon X_{t/\varepsilon^2}\Rightarrow\pi^{-1/4}B_t\) for \(\mathbf P\)-a.e. realization of \(f\), where \(B\) is a standard Brownian motion

Precise statement

Hypotheses in the critical case. Let \(d\ge2\), \(\nu\in(0,1]\), and \(\square_n=(-3^n/2,3^n/2)^d\). Let \(\mathbf j_n:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R_{\mathrm{skew}}^{d\times d}\), \(n\ge0\), be stationary random fields with joint law \(\mathbf P\). Define \(\mathbf k(x)-\mathbf k(y)=\sum_{n\ge0}(\mathbf j_n(x)-\mathbf j_n(y))\) and \(f=\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf k=\sum_{n\ge0}\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf j_n\). Let \(X_0=0\) and let \(X\) have generator \(\mathcal L=\nabla\!\cdot(\nu I+\mathbf k)\nabla=\nu\Delta+f\cdot\nabla\). The assumptions are: (J1) \(\mathbf j_n\) has range of dependence \(3^n\sqrt d\); (J2) collections indexed by disjoint sets of scales are independent; (J3) \(\mathbf j_n\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1}\) almost surely and, for \(s\ge1\), \[\mathbf P\!\left[\|\mathbf j_n\|_{L^\infty(\square_n)}+\sqrt d\,3^n\|\nabla\mathbf j_n\|_{L^\infty(\square_n)}+d3^{2n}\|\nabla^2\mathbf j_n\|_{L^\infty(\square_n)}>s\right]\le e^{-s^2};\] (J4) the joint law is invariant under \(\mathbf j_n\mapsto-\mathbf j_n\) and under \(\mathbf j_n(x)\mapsto R^{\mathsf T}\mathbf j_n(Rx)R\) for every signed permutation matrix \(R\); and (J5) there are \(c_*,C_*\in(0,\infty)\) such that, for \(0\le n<m\) and \(e\in\partial B_1\), \[\left|\mathbf E\!\left[\left|\nabla\Delta^{-1}\!\left(\nabla\!\cdot\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}\mathbf j_\ell e\right)(0)\right|^2\right]-c_*(\log3)(m-n)\right|\le C_*.\]

Write \(M_f(t)=\mathbf E_0^f|X_t|^2\), where \(f\) is fixed and the expectation is over Brownian noise. Then \(M_f(t)/(t\sqrt{\log t})\to2dc_*^{1/2}\) in \(\mathbf P\)-probability. If \(W\) is standard \(d\)-dimensional Brownian motion, then, for \(\mathbf P\)-almost every \(f\), \[|\log\varepsilon^2|^{-1/4}\varepsilon X_{t/\varepsilon^2}\Longrightarrow\sqrt{2c_*^{1/2}}\,W_t\] in law with respect to the topology of locally uniform convergence on \(C([0,\infty);\mathbb R^d)\). In the two-dimensional special case in which \(f\) is \(\nabla^\perp\) of a mollified GFF, \(c_*=1/(4\pi)\), so the coefficient \(2dc_*^{1/2}\) equals \(2/\sqrt\pi\).

Proof idea. The proof is based on a rigorous renormalization group argument in which the coarse-grained diffusivities are analyzed inductively, scale by scale: on length scales of order \(3^m\), the generator is approximated by \(\bar s_m\Delta\), where the renormalized diffusivity \(\bar s_m\) is close to \((2c_*\log3^m)^{1/2}\). This gives sharp homogenization and large-scale regularity estimates on the infinitesimal generator, which are transferred into quantitative information on the process; since the process is Feller, convergence of generators gives convergence of the processes.

Hypotheses for positive Hurst exponent. Let \(d\ge2\), \(\gamma\in(0,1/4]\), \(\nu>0\), \(\square_0=(-1/2,1/2)^d\), and let \((\mathbf j_k)_{k\in\mathbb Z}\) be independent antisymmetric matrix fields satisfying \(\mathbf j_k\overset{\mathrm{law}}=3^{\gamma k}\mathbf j_0(3^{-k}\cdot)\). Assume: (J1) \(\mathbf j_0\) is mean zero, stationary, and has range of dependence \(\sqrt d\); (J2) \(\mathbf j_0\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1}\) almost surely and, for \(s\ge1\), \[\mathbf P\!\left[\|\mathbf j_0\|_{L^\infty(\square_0)}+\sqrt d\,\|\nabla\mathbf j_0\|_{L^\infty(\square_0)}+d\|\nabla^2\mathbf j_0\|_{L^\infty(\square_0)}>s\right]\le e^{-s^2};\] (J3) the joint law of \((\mathbf j_k)_{k\in\mathbb Z}\) is invariant under \(\mathbf j_k\mapsto-\mathbf j_k\) and under \(\mathbf j_k(x)\mapsto R^{\mathsf T}\mathbf j_k(Rx)R\) for every signed permutation matrix \(R\); and (J4) there is \(c_*>0\) such that \(\mathbf E[|(\nabla\Delta^{-1}\nabla\!\cdot(\mathbf j_0e))(0)|^2]=c_*\log3\) for every \(e\in\partial B_1\). Let \(\mathbf k=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\mathbf j_k\) modulo constant antisymmetric matrices, and let \(X\) have generator \(\nabla\!\cdot(\nu I+\mathbf k)\nabla\).

Theorem (quenched power-law superdiffusivity). Define \[R(t)=\left((\nu t)^{2-\gamma}+c_*\gamma^{-1}t^2\right)^{1/[2(2-\gamma)]}.\] For fixed \(\mathbf k\), put \(M_{\mathbf k}(t)=\mathbf E^{\mathbf k,0}|X_t|^2\). There are \(\gamma_0=\gamma_0(d,c_*)>0\) and \(C=C(d,c_*)<\infty\) such that, if \(0<\gamma\le\gamma_0\), then, for every \(t>0\) and \(1\le p\le C^{-1}\gamma^{-1}|\log\gamma|^{-6}\), \[\mathbf E\!\left[|M_{\mathbf k}(t)-2dR(t)^2|^p\right]^{1/p}\le C\bigl(p^{1/2}+|\log\gamma|^{1/2}\bigr)\gamma^{1/2}|\log\gamma|^4R(t)^2,\] \[\mathbf E\!\left[|\mathbf E^{\mathbf k,0}X_t|^{2p}\right]^{1/p}\le C\bigl(p+|\log\gamma|\bigr)\gamma|\log\gamma|^7R(t)^2.\] The outer expectation is over \(\mathbf k\). With \(t_*=(c_*^{-1}\gamma\nu^{2-\gamma})^{1/\gamma}\), \[R(t)\sim(c_*/\gamma)^{1/[2(2-\gamma)]}t^{1/(2-\gamma)}\qquad(t/t_*\to\infty).\]

Brownian motion in a log-correlated potential

A Brownian particle in \(\mathbb R^d\), \(d\ge2\), whose drift is the gradient of a log-correlated random potential.

The potential has independent fluctuations of equal strength at every length scale. The drift pushes the particle toward the large values of the potential, where the reversible measure puts its mass, and to travel the particle must cross the barriers of the energy landscape given by minus the potential. As more scales contribute, their effects accumulate and the effective diffusivity tends to zero. The particle is then subdiffusive: the time it needs to travel a large distance \(r\) grows faster than \(r^2\).

The finite simulation

The numerical path approximates the solution in the plane of \(dX=\nabla P(X)\,dt+\sqrt2\,dW\) by the Euler–Maruyama scheme with step \(\Delta t=0.004\), starting at the origin. The potential is \(P=\sigma\sum_{k=1}^{4}g_k\), where the \(g_k\) are independent smooth random fields and \(g_k\) has the law of \(g_1(3^{1-k}\,\cdot)\); the three buttons set \(\sigma=1.2,\,2.4,\,3.6\). Definition. Let \(d\ge2\) and let \(\mathsf g_0,\mathsf g_1,\mathsf g_2,\ldots\) be independent random fields on \(\mathbb R^d\) such that \(\mathsf g_k\) has the law of \(\mathsf g_0(3^{-k}\,\cdot)\). The field \(\mathsf g_0\) is stationary, has mean zero and range of dependence \(\sqrt d\), is \(C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}\), and its size is controlled by the disorder strength \(\delta\in(0,1/2]\); the precise assumptions are (g1)–(g4) of the paper. Set \[\mathbf a(x)=\exp\Big(\sum_{k\ge0}\big(\mathsf g_k(x)-\mathsf g_k(0)\big)\Big).\] The particle solves \[dX_t=\nabla\log\mathbf a(X_t)\,dt+\sqrt2\,dW_t,\] where \(W\) is a standard Brownian motion. The diffusion has generator \(\mathbf a^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\mathbf a\nabla)\) and reversible measure \(\mathbf a(x)\,dx\), and \(\log\mathbf a\) is log-correlated: the variance of \(\log\mathbf a(x)-\log\mathbf a(y)\) lies between two positive multiples of \(\log|x-y|\) for all sufficiently large \(|x-y|\). The environment is a realization of the fields; the law of the trajectories in a fixed environment is called quenched, and its average over the environment annealed.

Theorem. Assume (g1)–(g4). There is \(\delta_0(d)>0\) such that, if \(0<\delta\le\delta_0\), the following holds. Let \((\mathsf g_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\) be independent, with \(\mathsf g_j\) having the law of \(\mathsf g_0(3^{-j}\,\cdot)\). There is a family of quenched path laws, measurably determined by \((\mathsf g_j)_{j\in\mathbb Z}\), which almost surely defines a continuous strong Markov process \(Z\) with the following properties. For every \(x\), as the integer \(N\to\infty\), the annealed law of \(\big(3^{-N}X_{3^{2N}t/\bar{\mathbf a}_N}\big)_{t\ge0}\) with \(X_0=3^Nx\) converges to the annealed law of \(Z\) started at \(x\), where \(\bar{\mathbf a}_N\) is the effective diffusivity at scale \(3^N\). Almost surely, \(Z\) is reversible with respect to a measure that is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure. There are \(C<\infty\) and \(\eta>0\), depending only on \(d\) and the law of \(\mathsf g_0\), such that, for every starting point and every \(r\in(0,1]\), the annealed mean time for \(Z\) to leave the cube of side \(r\) centered at its starting point is at most \(Cr^{2+\eta}\); in dimension two one can take \(\eta=\tau^2/\log3\), where \(\tau^2=\log\mathbf E[e^{\mathsf g_0(0)}]\). (Armstrong–Bou-Rabee–Kuusi, Theorem A)

Consequently, for every \(x\), the annealed path law of \(Z\) started at \(x\), and almost surely its quenched path law, is mutually singular with every mixture of Brownian laws with arbitrary covariance matrices. (Theorem 7.1(iii))

The effective diffusivity

Keeping only the fields \(\mathsf g_0,\ldots,\mathsf g_L\) gives a diffusion \(X^{(L)}\), and its effective diffusivity \(\bar{\mathbf a}_L\) is characterized by the annealed scaling limit \(\bigl(\varepsilon X^{(L)}_{t/\varepsilon^2}\bigr)_{t\ge0}\to\bigl(\sqrt{2\bar{\mathbf a}_L}\,W_t\bigr)_{t\ge0}\) in law as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), with \(X^{(L)}\) started at the origin. These diffusivities are nonincreasing in \(L\), and \(e^{-(L+1)\tau^2}\le\bar{\mathbf a}_L\le3^{-\eta_{\mathrm{sub}}(L+1)}\) for every \(L\ge0\), for some \(\eta_{\mathrm{sub}}>0\) depending only on \(d\) and the law of \(\mathsf g_0\); in dimension two \(\bar{\mathbf a}_L=e^{-(L+1)\tau^2}\) exactly. When \(\mathsf g_0\) is Gaussian, the reversible measure of \(Z\) is Gaussian multiplicative chaos. In the incompressible models of Brownian motion in an incompressible random drift, the effective diffusivity increases with scale; here it decreases.

Definition. On a probability space \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb Q)\), let \(d\ge2\), \(\Lambda\ge1\), let \(r_0\ge1\) be an integer, let \(\mathbf a\) be a symmetric positive-definite matrix field, and let \(V\) be a scalar field. Assume \((\mathbf a,V)\) is stationary with finite range of dependence \(r_0\), \(\Lambda^{-1}|\xi|^2\le\xi\cdot\mathbf a(x)\xi\le\Lambda|\xi|^2\), \(|V(x)|\le\Lambda\), and both fields are \(\Lambda\)-Lipschitz. The unforced generator is \(\mathcal Lf=\tfrac12e^{2V}\nabla\!\cdot(e^{-2V}\mathbf a\nabla f)\). For \(\mathbb Q\)-almost every environment, \(\varepsilon X_{t/\varepsilon^2}\Rightarrow W_t\) in law, where \(\mathbf E[W_t\otimes W_t]=t\Sigma_X\) for a deterministic effective diffusivity \(\Sigma_X\). Applying a constant force of magnitude \(\lambda\in(0,1]\) in the direction \(e_1\) gives \(\mathcal L^\lambda f=\mathcal Lf+\lambda\mathbf a e_1\!\cdot\nabla f\). The resulting biased diffusion \(X^\lambda\), started at the origin, satisfies a quenched law of large numbers: for almost every environment, \(X_t^\lambda/t\to\ell_X(\lambda)\) almost surely, for a deterministic effective velocity \(\ell_X(\lambda)\).

Theorem. Under the hypotheses above, there is a deterministic \(\beta^*=\beta^*(d)\in(0,1/4)\) and a random scale \(\lambda_0:\Omega\to(0,1]\) such that, \(\mathbb Q\)-almost surely, \[\left|\frac{\ell_X(\lambda)}{\lambda}-\Sigma_Xe_1\right|\le\lambda^{\beta^*}\qquad(0<\lambda\le\lambda_0).\] There are \(C,c>0\) and \(\sigma\in(0,1)\), depending only on \((d,\Lambda,r_0)\), such that \(\mathbb Q(\lambda_0\le\lambda)\le C\exp[-c(\log(1/\lambda))^\sigma]\) for every \(\lambda\in(0,1]\). (Bou-Rabee–Xu, arXiv:2605.26082, Theorem 2)

Question. Prove the conjectured sharp rate \(\lambda\) for \(d\ge3\) and \(\lambda\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}\) for \(d=2\).

Details

Proof idea. Under a force of magnitude \(\lambda\), the ballistic displacement \(\lambda t\) and the diffusive fluctuation \(\sqrt t\) are comparable precisely when \(t\asymp\lambda^{-2}\), that is, on spatial scales of order \(\lambda^{-1}\). A time change absorbs the potential into the divergence-form coefficient \(e^{-2V}\mathbf a\). On a cube of side slightly larger than \(\lambda^{-1}\), the solution of a resolvent equation, via the Feynman–Kac formula, encodes the mobility error as an exponentially weighted average of the path.

This average splits into four contributions: short-time, long-time, exit, and homogenization. The short-time and exit terms are controlled directly; the long-time term by a law of large numbers uniform in \(\lambda\), and the homogenization term by a quantitative estimate for the tilted resolvent. Optimizing the free parameters in the resulting bound yields the algebraic rate \(\lambda^{\beta_*}\).

A. Bou-Rabee and R. Xu, “Quantitative Einstein relation for reversible diffusions in a random environment” (2026), arXiv:2605.26082.

Random walk on orthogonal tilings

Definition. Cover \(D\subseteq\mathbb R^d\) by closed, convex polytopes \(P_v\) with disjoint interiors, each associated with a vertex \(v\) in its interior, such that each compact subset of \(D\) intersects finitely many polytopes. Vertices \(v\) and \(w\) are joined by an edge, \(v\sim w\), if \(P_v\cap P_w\) is a \((d-1)\)-dimensional facet. The tiling is orthogonal if, for each edge, \(w-v\) is orthogonal to the hyperplane containing \(P_v\cap P_w\). Put \(a(v,w)=\operatorname{vol}_{d-1}(P_v\cap P_w)/\lVert v-w\rVert\), \(a(v)=\sum_{w\sim v}a(v,w)\), and \(p(v,w)=a(v,w)/a(v)\).

Proof mechanism

The passage time

1 · boundary routing

Florescu, Levine and Peres (2016) showed that, if \(T(n)<\infty\), then between \(T(n)\) and \(T(n+1)\) every directed edge is used at most once, and that, if \(T(n+1)<\infty\), the walk departs exactly \(\deg(x)\) times from every \(x\in A_n\). For a finite set \(S\) of vertices, place one particle at the head of each directed edge leaving \(S\), declare \(S\) absorbing, and move the particles by the rotor rule until every particle has stepped into \(S\). When this procedure terminates, let \(\Phi(S)\) be the union of \(S\) and the set of vertices visited during it. The map \(\Phi\) is monotone, and if \(T(n+1)<\infty\), then

\[A_{n+1}=\Phi(A_n),\qquad A_n=\Phi^n(\{o\}).\]

2 · passage time

The passage time, the number of circuits required for the walk started at \(x\) to reach \(y\), \[\tau(x,y)=\min\{n:y\in\Phi^n(\{x\})\}\] satisfies the triangle inequality, is stationary in law, and is at most the graph distance. The subadditive ergodic theorem provides a deterministic limit \(\tau(o,nz)/n\to\mu(z)\), and the unit ball of \(\mu\) is the limit shape \(B\). The almost-live-path estimate, stated below, shows that \(\mu\) does not vanish.

3 · circuit clock

Write \(d(S)=\sum_{x\in S}\deg(x)\). If \(T(n)<\infty\) for every \(n\), then, for every \(n\),

\[d(A_n)\le T(n+1)-T(n)\le d(A_{n+1}).\]

If, in addition, \(|A_n|=\alpha n^2+o(n^2)\) and \(d(A_n)=\beta n^2+o(n^2)\) for some \(\alpha,\beta>0\), then

\[T(n)=\frac{\beta}{3}n^3+o(n^3).\]

Moreover \(|R_t|t^{-2/3}\to\alpha(3/\beta)^{2/3}\), and if \(n^{-1}A_n\to B\) in Hausdorff distance, then \(t^{-1/3}R_t\to(3/\beta)^{1/3}B\).

Details

Live paths. A path \(x_0,x_1,\ldots\) of distinct vertices is live at an internal vertex \(x_i\) if, in the cyclic order beginning immediately after the initial rotor at \(x_i\), the edge \(x_i\to x_{i+1}\) occurs before \(x_i\to x_{i-1}\). If \(y\in\Phi^n(\{x\})\), then some path from \(x\) to \(y\) fails the live condition at no more than \(n-1\) of its internal vertices. The almost-live-path estimate states that, for some \(\eta>0\), the probability that some path starting with a given directed edge \(u\to v\) reaches graph distance \(R\) from \(u\) while the live condition fails at no more than \(\eta R\) internal vertices tends to zero as \(R\to\infty\), uniformly in \(u\to v\).

Square lattice. Rotating a directed edge \(v\to w\) counterclockwise through \(90^\circ\) about its midpoint gives a dual edge, called open when \(v\to w\) comes second or third in the cyclic order beginning after the initial rotor at \(v\). The four dual edges at a vertex form a square, of which two adjacent sides are open; no open dual path traverses a fixed five-edge pattern, and percolation avoiding that pattern is subcritical. The sides of a square are not independent, so the estimate is transferred to the rotor model by an exploration.

Theorem. Fix \(d\ge2\). Let \(\omega_d\) be the volume of the Euclidean unit ball, put \(B_r=\{x\in\mathbb Z^d:|x|_2<r\}\), and set \(r_n=(n/\omega_d)^{1/d}\). Theorem. For each fixed \(d\ge2\), there exists a deterministic \(C=C(d)<\infty\) such that, almost surely, for all sufficiently large \(r\), \[B_{r-C\log r}\subset A_{\lfloor\omega_2r^2\rfloor}\subset B_{r+C\log r}\qquad(d=2),\] and \[B_{r-C\sqrt{\log r}}\subset A_{\lfloor\omega_dr^d\rfloor}\subset B_{r+C\sqrt{\log r}}\qquad(d\ge3).\] In continuous-time planar IDLA, let \(T(t)\) be an independent rate-one Poisson process, let \(F(x)=\inf\{t:x\in A_{T(t)}\}\), \(L(x)=\sqrt{F(x)/\pi}-|x|\), and \(G_R(z)=L(\lfloor Rz\rfloor)\). For a test function \(\phi\), define \((\phi,G_R)=\int_{\mathbb R^2}\phi(z)G_R(z)\,dz\). For every finite collection \(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_m\) in \(H_0=\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{Re}(az^k)f(|z|):a\in\mathbb C,\ k\ge0,\ f\in C_c^\infty((0,\infty))\}\), the vector \(((\phi_j,G_R))_{j=1}^m\) converges in distribution as \(R\to\infty\) to the corresponding vector of pairings with the planar augmented Gaussian free field. (Jerison–Levine–Sheffield, arXiv:1010.2483, arXiv:1012.3453 and arXiv:1101.0596)

Question. Is the logarithmic bound on the maximal fluctuations sharp in dimension \(d=2\)?

Details

Proof idea. The connection between IDLA and the divisible sandpile was implicit in the first work of Lawler, Bramson and Griffeath: a lower bound on the divisible sandpile cluster implies the same lower bound for the IDLA cluster. The logarithmic maximal fluctuation bounds use martingales built from discrete harmonic functions, an estimate excluding thin tentacles, and an argument controlling early and late points. For the fluctuation theorem, sums of discrete harmonic polynomials over the cluster are martingales; their quadratic variations converge, and a martingale central limit theorem gives the augmented Gaussian free field.

Uniform spanning tree

Definition. On a finite connected graph, Wilson’s algorithm maintains a current tree, which initially consists of just the root. While there remain vertices not in the tree, it runs a simple random walk from the first such vertex in a fixed order of the vertices, erasing cycles as they are created, until the walk encounters the current tree; then the cycle-erased trajectory is added to the current tree.

Theorem. Let \(D\subsetneq\mathbb C\) be a simply connected domain with \(0\in D\). For \(\delta>0\), let \(\mu_\delta\) be the law of the loop-erasure of simple random walk on the grid \(\delta\mathbb Z^2\), started at 0 and stopped when it hits \(\partial D\), and let \(\nu\) be the law of the image of the radial \(\mathrm{SLE}_2\) path under a conformal map from the unit disk to \(D\) fixing 0. Then \(\mu_\delta\) converges weakly to \(\nu\) as \(\delta\to0\), with respect to the metric \(\rho(\beta,\gamma)=\inf\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\hat\beta(t)-\hat\gamma(t)|\) on unparameterized paths, where the infimum is over all parameterizations \(\hat\beta,\hat\gamma\) of \(\beta,\gamma\) by \([0,1]\). (Lawler–Schramm–Werner, arXiv:math/0112234, Theorem 1.1)

Theorem. Let \(D\subset\mathbb C\) be a domain whose boundary is a \(C^1\)-smooth simple closed curve, let \(a,b\in\partial D\) be distinct, and let \(\alpha,\beta\) be the two complementary arcs of \(\partial D\) whose endpoints are \(a\) and \(b\). For \(\delta>0\), let \(\gamma_\delta\) be the Peano curve of the UST on an approximation of \(D\) in the grid \(\delta\mathbb Z^2\), with wired boundary near \(\alpha\) and free boundary near \(\beta\). Then \(\gamma_\delta\) converges weakly, with respect to \(\rho\), to the image of the chordal \(\mathrm{SLE}_8\) path under a conformal map from the upper half-plane to \(D\) mapping 0 to \(a\) and \(\infty\) to \(b\). (Lawler–Schramm–Werner, arXiv:math/0112234, Theorem 1.3)

Details

Proof idea. Wilson associates with each vertex other than the root an infinite stack of independent random successors and pops directed cycles off the stacks; the choice of which cycle to pop next is irrelevant, and the probability that the stacks define a tree \(T\) and a set of popped cycles factors into a term depending on the cycles alone and the weight of \(T\). Lawler, Schramm and Werner (2004) first show that the Loewner driving process of the discrete path converges to Brownian motion, using a Markovian property of the path and a martingale observable: for the loop-erased walk, the expected number of visits to a vertex \(v\) by the simple random walk generating it; for the Peano curve, the probability that the path in the UST from \(v\) to \(\alpha\) hits a given subarc of \(\alpha\). Regularity properties of the discrete paths then give convergence in the metric \(\rho\).

D. B. Wilson, “Generating random spanning trees more quickly than the cover time”, STOC (1996). G. Lawler, O. Schramm and W. Werner, “Conformal invariance of planar loop-erased random walks and uniform spanning trees”, Ann. Probab. 32 (2004), arXiv:math/0112234. The loop-erased walk is due to G. Lawler (1980). A. Broder (1989) and D. Aldous (1990) found an earlier random-walk algorithm for uniform spanning trees.

Rotor-router aggregation

Definition. Fix \(d\ge2\), a cyclic order of \(\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_d\}\), and an initial rotor at every site of \(\mathbb Z^d\). Set \(A_0=\varnothing\). For \(n\ge1\), release a particle at the origin; while it is in \(A_{n-1}\), the rotor at its current location is rotated to the next direction in the cyclic order, and the particle takes a step in the direction of the newly rotated rotor. At the first site \(z_n\notin A_{n-1}\), stop the particle and set \(A_n=A_{n-1}\cup\{z_n\}\). The displayed run has \(d=2\), clockwise rotor order, and all rotors initially east.

Observation. Take one simulated cluster of 1,200,000 particles for each of rotor-router aggregation and internal DLA in \(\mathbb Z^2\), so that \(r=(n/\pi)^{1/2}=618.04\). Divide the plane into 720 equal angular sectors about the origin, and in each sector let \(r(\theta)\) be the largest distance from the origin of an occupied site. Over the 720 sectors, \(r(\theta)\) has standard deviation 0.53 and range 2.35 for rotor-router aggregation, and 0.98 and 6.58 for internal DLA; for the lattice disk \(\{x:|x|\le r\}\) the values are 0.16 and 0.83.

Question. Do there exist constants \(c,c'\) depending only on \(d\) such that \(B_{r-c}\subset A_n\subset B_{r+c'}\) for every \(n\)?

Details

Proof idea. Let \(u(x)\) be the total number of exits from \(x\) by the first \(n\) particles. Its gradient along an edge differs from \(-2d\) times the net number of crossings of that edge by at most \(4d-2\), and estimates on the Green's function for simple random walk stopped on exiting \(B_r\) then give \(u>0\) on \(B_{r-c\log r}\). For the outer bound, each particle is stopped when it reaches an unoccupied site or the spherical shell \(\mathcal S_\rho\) of radius \(\rho\); the particles stopped on the shell are restarted using the abelian property, and when \(h\) is of order \(r^{1-1/d}\) a constant fraction of the particles that reach \(\mathcal S_\rho\) find unoccupied sites before reaching \(\mathcal S_{\rho+h}\).

Rotor-router walk is a deterministic analogue of random walk, first studied by Priezzhev, Dhar, Dhar and Krishnamurthy (1996) under the name “Eulerian walkers”; rotor-router aggregation was introduced by Propp. Internal DLA is the analogous growth model defined using random walks instead of rotor-router walks.

Integer superharmonic matrices on \(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\)

Definition. For \(k\in\mathbb Z\), let \(\mathcal B_k\) be the Apollonian circle packing generated by the vertical lines through \((2k,0)\) and \((2k+2,0)\) together with the circle of radius 1 centred at \((2k+1,0)\): the minimal collection of general circles (circles or lines) containing these three that is closed under the addition of Soddy general circles. The band packing is \(\mathcal B=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb Z}\mathcal B_k\). With \(\Delta g(x)=\sum_{y\sim x}(g(y)-g(x))\), a symmetric matrix \(A\) is integer superharmonic when an integer-valued \(g\) satisfies \(\Delta g\le1\) and \(g(x)=\tfrac12x\cdot Ax+o(|x|^2)\). Write \(A\preccurlyeq B\) when \(B-A\) is positive semidefinite. If \(C\in\mathcal B\) has centre \((x_1,x_2)\) and radius \(r\), then \(A_C=\tfrac12\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}r+x_1&x_2\\x_2&r-x_1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\).

Theorem. Let \(\mathcal B\) be the Apollonian band packing defined above. A symmetric matrix \(A\) is integer superharmonic on \(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\) precisely when \(\operatorname{tr}A\le0\), or \(A\preccurlyeq A_C\) for some circle \(C\in\mathcal B\). If \(C\) has centre \((x_1,x_2)\) and radius \(r\), then \[A_C=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}r+x_1&x_2\\x_2&r-x_1\end{pmatrix}.\] For each circle \(C\in\mathcal B\), with \(L_C=\{v\in\mathbb Z^2:A_Cv\in\mathbb Z^2\}\), there is an integer superharmonic representative \(g_C\) for \(A_C\) with \(g_C(x+v)=g_C(x)+x^{\mathsf T}A_Cv+g_C(v)\) for all \(v\in L_C\) and \(x\in\mathbb Z^2\), which is maximal in the sense that \(g-g_C\) is bounded whenever \(g:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z\) satisfies \(\Delta g\le1\) and \(g\ge g_C\); and there is a tile \(T_C\) with \(90^\circ\) rotational symmetry such that \(T_C+L_C\) is a tiling of \(\mathbb Z^2\). The curvature of \(C\) is the area of \(T_C\). (Levine–Pegden–Smart, arXiv:1309.3267, Theorems 1.1–1.3)

Question. Describe the set of integer superharmonic matrices for periodically embedded graphs other than \(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\).

Details

Proof idea. For each circle \(C\in\mathcal B\), construct a tile \(T_C\) that tiles the plane periodically and decomposes into copies of the tiles of the parent circles of \(C\) with specified overlaps. Use the decomposition of \(T_C\) to define recursively a tile odometer on \(T_C\). Extend the tile odometer to \(\mathbb Z^2\) via the periodicity condition, and check that the extension \(g_C\) satisfies \(\Delta g_C\le1\) and is maximal.

The condition \(\operatorname{tr}A\le0\) in the theorem adds a Cantor set of matrices in the plane \(\operatorname{tr}A=0\) that lie in the topological closure of the downset of \(\{A_C:C\in\mathcal B\}\) but not in the downset itself.
