Site percolation at criticality

ChatGPT 5.6 Sol and Claude Fable 5.1, prompted by Ahmed Bou-Rabee

In Bernoulli site percolation on \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), each site is open with probability \(p\) and closed with probability \(1-p\), independently of the other sites. An open cluster is a set of open sites any two of which are joined by a path of open sites, and \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}(p)\) is the probability that the origin lies in an infinite open cluster. It is nondecreasing in \(p\), and \(p_c\) is the parameter above which it is positive.

By construction \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}\) vanishes below \(p_c\) and is positive above it. That leaves exactly one value undetermined, the one at \(p_c\) itself, where an infinite cluster is neither forced nor forbidden by the definition. Whether \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}(p_c)=0\) is the question.

For \(d = 2\) the answer is yes. For bond percolation it is Harris and Kesten; for site percolation it is Russo, whose 1981 paper builds on Kesten's determination of the bond threshold on the square lattice, and proves that \(\theta\) is continuous on all of \([0,1]\). For \(d \ge 3\) it is not known in general.

For bond percolation the answer is now yes in every dimension. Anthropic's Lean 4 formalization proves \(\theta(p_c)=0\) on \(\mathbb{Z}^d\) for all \(d \ge 2\), settling the cases \(3 \le d \le 10\) that were open, by proving a conjectured inequality of Kozma and Nitzan about finite graphs. The companion page proves the rest of the conjectures and questions of their paper on top of it. This page is the site percolation extension, and it builds on both.

The two models

What is proved

One finite inequality covers both models. Bond percolation is the case in which every label of a hypergraph is incident to two vertices; site percolation is the case in which each site carries one label. Formally, with HyperedgeGluing the inequality for hypergraphs, Conjecture1 the Kozma–Nitzan bond inequality and SiteGluingUnpinned its site analogue, \[ \texttt{HyperedgeGluing} \;\Longrightarrow\; \texttt{Conjecture1} \;\wedge\; \texttt{SiteGluingUnpinned}, \] machine-checked. So the finite input is one theorem, not two. It has to be stated with labels rather than with the vertex records that suffice for graphs, and the files contain a machine-checked counterexample showing why: two distinct labels can have the same outside trace, so intersections are lost.

Proved

  • The representation. Site percolation on any finite graph is the hypergraph model in which each site carries one label, incident to the site and to one further vertex per edge at it. The labels are the sites, so the product measure over labels is the site measure itself.
  • The conditioning apparatus. Conditioning on the entire cluster of a source set factorizes exactly; two disjoint clusters can be factorized in either order; and any probability expands as a finite sum over the possible clusters.
  • Monotonicity and nondegeneracy. The percolation probability is monotone in the parameter and in the underlying graph, it vanishes below \(p_c\), and \(0 < p_c < 1\), the upper bound coming from a half-edge comparison with bond percolation.
  • The endgame. If percolation at a parameter forces percolation in a slab of finite width at some strictly smaller parameter, then \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}(p_c)=0\). This step is self-contained: it assumes no theorem about percolation at a critical point, imports nothing, and carries no side conditions.

Not yet proved

Two statements, and only these two, stand between the above and an unconditional theorem.

  • The hyperedge inequality. In a finite hypergraph whose labels are open independently, for a nonempty set \(A\) of vertices and vertices \(o\) and \(b\), \[ \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow A)\,\min_{a \in A}\mathbb{P}(a \leftrightarrow b) \;\le\; \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b,\; o \leftrightarrow A). \]
  • The slab reduction. If \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}(p) > 0\) then site percolation occurs in a slab of finite width, at the same \(p\) or below it. Grimmett and Marstrand prove this with \(p + \gamma\) in place of \(p\) for every \(\gamma > 0\), and name the passage to \(p\) itself as the point where their argument fails, because it adds extra open sites to build the paths it needs. Replacing that addition by the hyperedge inequality is what the route is for.

Both are named hypotheses in the files, so every theorem that uses them displays them. Nothing else is assumed on this route.

The second endgame, and what it costs

There is a second endgame, which takes the slab reduction at the same \(p\) rather than below it and pays for the difference with two theorems from the literature. Barsky, Grimmett and Newman prove that the percolation probability of a half-space vanishes at the critical density of the half-space, for bond or site percolation. Grimmett and Marstrand prove that for \(d \ge 3\) that density equals the one of \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), working throughout with site percolation. Both are needed: the first alone places the statement at the wrong critical parameter.

So the two routes differ exactly in what they assume. The first is conditional on the slab reduction below the parameter, and on nothing else whatsoever. The second is conditional on the slab reduction at the same parameter together with both published theorems. Neither route is conditional on just one of them.

The two published theorems are carried here as named hypotheses and are not formalized on this page. That is a gap in the formalization, not in the mathematics: they are theorems, and the bond case of the half-space theorem is already proved from scratch in Anthropic's Lean development, so a site version is a port of an existing machine-checked argument rather than open mathematics.
