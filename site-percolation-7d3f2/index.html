Site percolation at criticality

ChatGPT 5.6 Sol and Claude Fable 5.1, prompted by Ahmed Bou-Rabee

In Bernoulli site percolation on \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), each site is open with probability \(p\) and closed with probability \(1-p\), independently of the other sites. An open cluster is a set of open sites any two of which are joined by a path of open sites, and \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}(p)\) is the probability that the origin lies in an infinite open cluster. It is nondecreasing in \(p\), and \(p_c\) is the parameter above which it is positive. Whether \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}(p_c)=0\) is the question treated here.

For \(d = 2\) the answer is yes, by Russo. For \(d \ge 3\) it is not known. The companion page treats the same question for bond percolation, where each edge rather than each site is open with probability \(p\); there \(\theta(p_c)=0\) holds in every dimension \(d \ge 2\).

The two models

What is proved in Lean

A route to \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}(p_c)=0\) on \(\mathbb{Z}^d\) for \(d \ge 3\) has been proposed, passing through one inequality about finite hypergraphs. The Lean files state that route and prove every step of it that is not a theorem of the literature. Two statements are open, and both are named hypotheses, so any theorem using them displays them:

  • The hyperedge inequality. In a finite hypergraph whose labels are open independently, for a nonempty set \(A\) of vertices and vertices \(o\) and \(b\), \[ \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow A)\,\min_{a \in A}\mathbb{P}(a \leftrightarrow b) \;\le\; \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b,\; o \leftrightarrow A). \]
  • The same-parameter slab reduction. If \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}(p) > 0\) then site percolation occurs at the same \(p\) in a slab of finite width. Grimmett and Marstrand prove this with \(p + \gamma\) in place of \(p\), for every \(\gamma > 0\), and name the passage to \(p\) itself as the point where their argument fails, because it adds a small density of extra open sites to build the paths it needs. Replacing that addition by the hyperedge inequality is what the proposed route is for.

The remaining input is a theorem, in two published pieces, each covering site percolation:

  • Barsky, Grimmett and Newman prove that the percolation probability of a half-space vanishes at the critical density of the half-space, for independent nearest-neighbour bond or site percolation.
  • Grimmett and Marstrand prove that for \(d \ge 3\) the critical density of the half-space equals that of \(\mathbb{Z}^d\). That paper works throughout with site percolation on \(\mathbb{Z}^d\).

The two are stated separately in Lean, since neither alone gives the statement the route uses.

What the files prove:

  • The representation. Site percolation on any finite graph is the hypergraph model in which each site carries one label, and that label is incident to the site itself and to one further vertex for each edge at the site. The labels are the sites, so the product measure over labels is the site measure itself, and connection in the hypergraph is connection in the site model. The hyperedge inequality therefore gives the same inequality for site percolation on every finite graph.
  • Monotonicity in the underlying graph. Restricting a configuration along an injection of vertex sets carries the site measure to the site measure, open paths to open paths, and infinite clusters to infinite clusters, so the percolation probability on an induced subgraph is at most the one on the whole graph. This needs the event that the open cluster is infinite to be measurable, which is proved from the decomposition of a connection into open walks.
  • The half-space theorem gives the slab statement. A slab is contained in a half-space, and both are induced from \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), so no slab percolates at the critical parameter.
  • The conclusion. The half-space theorem and the slab reduction together give \(\theta_{\mathrm{site}}(p_c) = 0\) on \(\mathbb{Z}^d\).
  • A second endgame, with no imported theorem at all. If the reduction is strengthened to produce slab percolation at some parameter strictly below \(p\), the conclusion follows from the definition of the critical parameter alone: a slab lies in \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), so the lattice would percolate below its own critical parameter. This route uses no theorem about percolation at a critical point, and it needs neither of the two published theorems above.

The critical parameter is proved to lie in \([0,1]\), so the conclusion is an equation between two real numbers and holds under no hypothesis. Six further lemmas fix the conventions of the model: a closed site is connected to nothing, not even to itself; the cluster of a closed site is empty; every site of a cluster is open; and two adjacent open sites are connected.
