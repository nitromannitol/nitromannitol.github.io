Percolation at criticality

ChatGPT 5.6 Sol and Claude Fable 5.1, prompted by Ahmed Bou-Rabee

Whether critical Bernoulli percolation has an infinite cluster is a classical question, settled for bond percolation on \(\mathbb{Z}^d\) in every dimension by Anthropic's Lean proof of Kozma and Nitzan's Conjecture 3 (arXiv:2401.12397). This page collects two pieces of work built on top of that proof.

The Kozma–Nitzan conjectures and questions

Their paper leaves eight further numbered items open. All of them are settled, and each statement is machine-checked in Lean with the standard axioms and no sorry:

  • Conjectures 1, 2 and 4, in stronger forms than stated: the pre-FKG inequality keeps the event \(\{o \leftrightarrow A\}\) on both sides, and the minimising vertex in Conjecture 4 may be named before that event is imposed. Conjecture 3 follows from Conjecture 1.
  • Conjecture 6, without either of its two endpoint hypotheses, one of which turns out to be a consequence of Conjecture 1.
  • Questions 5, 7 and 9, affirmatively. Question 5 gives one coefficient vector that works for every target at once; Question 9 holds for every minimising vertex.
  • Question 8 is false as printed. On the path \(o - a_1 - a_2 - b\) with vertex probabilities \(1, 1/2, 1\) both vertices of \(A\) minimise, and one of them fails the inequality.

The method is a comparison for increasing functions of a cluster that remains valid when the cluster is conditioned to avoid a prescribed set, together with the same comparison for the union of the clusters of several vertices. Proofs are in the summary; the Lean modules are in lean/.

Site percolation

A separate manuscript, Critical Bernoulli site percolation on integer lattices, sets out to prove the corresponding statement for site percolation: that for every \(d \ge 2\) the origin almost surely lies in a finite open cluster at the critical parameter. Its finite input is a gluing inequality for independent hyperedges, which contains site percolation once each open site is replaced by one hyperedge joining its incident ports; a site version of the Kozma–Nitzan block construction then produces a percolating slab at the same parameter, which the half-space theorem excludes at criticality.

This manuscript is a draft. It has not been refereed, and unlike the results above it is not machine-checked: what exists in Lean so far is the statement of the theorem and of its finite input (SiteStatements.lean), not their proofs. Two independent audits are in progress, directed at the two parts the manuscript itself identifies as most in need of checking: the hyperedge extension of the conditioned covariance hierarchy, and the site exploration in the lattice argument.
