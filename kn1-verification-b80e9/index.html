The conjectures and questions of Kozma and Nitzan

Kozma and Nitzan (arXiv:2401.12397) reduced the statement \(\theta(p_c) = 0\) for Bernoulli bond percolation on \(\mathbb{Z}^d\), \(d \ge 2\), to an inequality on finite graphs, their Conjecture 3, and posed four further conjectures and four questions about connection probabilities in finite graphs: Conjectures 1, 2, 4 and 6 and Questions 5, 7, 8 and 9. The Lean 4 / Mathlib development θ(pc) = 0 for Bernoulli bond percolation on ℤd in all dimensions d ≥ 2 (Claude, Anthropic, under the direction of Justin Leder) proves Conjecture 3. It does not claim Conjecture 1, but Conjecture 1 is a relatively easy consequence of an inequality it proves on the way, as explained below.

Here the remaining conjectures and questions of the paper are proved on top of that development: Conjectures 1, 2, 4 and 6 and Questions 5, 7 and 9, each in a stronger form than stated. Question 8, as printed, has a negative answer when its minimiser is not unique, and is open otherwise. The proofs are in the summary (PDF); the Lean files are in lean/.

Notation

Throughout, \(G\) is a finite graph in which each edge \(e\) is open independently with probability \(p_e \in [0,1]\). For vertices \(x\) and \(y\), \(x \leftrightarrow y\) means that \(x\) and \(y\) are joined by a path of open edges, and \(C_x\) is the set of vertices joined to \(x\), the open cluster of \(x\). For a set \(A\) of vertices, \(o \leftrightarrow A\) means that \(o \leftrightarrow x\) for some \(x \in A\), and \(o \nleftrightarrow A\) is its negation. A comma inside a probability means intersection, \(\mathbb{P}(E, F) = \mathbb{P}(E \cap F)\), and \(\mathbb{E}[X;\, E]\) is the expectation of \(X\) on the event \(E\). Increasing functions \(F\) of vertex sets are those with \(F(K) \le F(L)\) whenever \(K \subseteq L\). Page numbers refer to the paper.

Conjecture 1 from the development

Fix \(A\), \(o\) and \(b\), and order the vertices of \(A\) so that \(\mathbb{P}(x \leftrightarrow b)\) is nondecreasing. The development proves \[ \sum_{x \in A} \mathbb{P}\bigl(o \leftrightarrow x \text{ and } o \nleftrightarrow y \text{ for every } y \in A \text{ before } x\bigr)\, \mathbb{P}(x \leftrightarrow b) \;\le\; \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b). \] The events on the left partition \(\{o \leftrightarrow A\}\), and every factor \(\mathbb{P}(x \leftrightarrow b)\) is at least the minimum over \(A\), so \[ \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow A)\, \min_{x \in A} \mathbb{P}(x \leftrightarrow b) \;\le\; \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b), \] which is Conjecture 1 (p. 3) for every finite graph and every choice of edge probabilities.

The results

  • Conjecture 4 (p. 32). Let \(F\) be an increasing function of vertex sets and let \(a \in A\) minimise \(\mathbb{E}\,F(C_x)\) over \(x \in A\). Then \(\mathbb{E}[F(C_a);\, o \leftrightarrow A] \le \mathbb{E}[F(C_o);\, o \leftrightarrow A]\). In particular \(\min_{x \in A} \mathbb{E}[F(C_x);\, o \leftrightarrow A] \le \mathbb{E}[F(C_o);\, o \leftrightarrow A]\), which is Conjecture 4; the same holds in the paper's formulation with a monotone cluster property \(f(v, \omega)\) in place of \(F(C_v)\).
  • Conjecture 2 (p. 3), in the pre-FKG form (3): \(\min_{x \in A} \mathbb{P}(x \leftrightarrow b,\, o \leftrightarrow A) \le \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b,\, o \leftrightarrow A)\). Hence \(\min_{x \in A} \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow A,\, x \leftrightarrow b) \le \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b)\), which is display (2).
  • Question 7 (p. 36), answered yes. Let \(a \in A\) minimise \(\mathbb{P}(x \leftrightarrow b)\) over \(x \in A\). Then \(\mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow A,\, a \leftrightarrow b) \le \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b,\, o \leftrightarrow A)\), which is display (41).
  • Conjecture 1 (p. 3), as above, and Conjecture 3 (p. 15) from it: for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is \(\delta > 0\) such that if \(\mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow A) > 1 - \delta\) and \(\mathbb{P}(x \leftrightarrow b) > 1 - \delta\) for every \(x \in A\), then \(\mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b) > 1 - \varepsilon\).
  • Question 5 (p. 32), answered yes. There are numbers \(c_a \ge 0\), \(a \in A\), with \(\sum_{a \in A} c_a = 1\), depending on \(G\), \(A\) and \(o\) but not on \(b\), such that \(\sum_{a \in A} c_a\, \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow A,\, a \leftrightarrow b) \le \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b)\) for every vertex \(b\), which is display (38).
  • Conjecture 6 (p. 34). Let \(e = \{v, w\}\) be an edge of \(G\), let \(a \in A\) minimise \(\mathbb{P}_G(x \leftrightarrow b)\) over \(x \in A\), and let \(G/e\) be the graph with the edge \(e\) shortened, that is, with the probability that \(e\) is open set to \(1\). Then \(\mathbb{P}_{G/e}(v \leftrightarrow b,\, v \leftrightarrow A) \ge \mathbb{P}_{G/e}(a \leftrightarrow b,\, v \leftrightarrow A)\), and therefore \(\mathbb{P}_{G/e}(v \leftrightarrow b) \ge \mathbb{P}_{G/e}(v \leftrightarrow A)\, \mathbb{P}_{G/e}(a \leftrightarrow b)\), which is display (40). The paper assumes in addition its display (39); that assumption is not needed, and it is in any case a consequence of Conjecture 1.
  • Question 9 (p. 36), answered yes. Let \(H\) be \(G\) with every edge at \(o\) removed and let \(a \in A\) minimise \(\mathbb{P}_H(x \leftrightarrow b)\) over \(x \in A\). Then \(\mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow A,\, a \leftrightarrow b) \le \mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b,\, o \leftrightarrow A)\) in \(G\).
  • Question 8 (p. 36) asks the same for a vertex \(a \in A\) minimising \(\mathbb{P}(x \leftrightarrow b,\, o \nleftrightarrow A)\). If \(\mathbb{P}(o \nleftrightarrow A) = 0\) then every vertex of \(A\) is a minimiser, and the inequality can fail: on the path \(o - a_1 - a_2 - b\) with edge probabilities \(1\), \(1/2\), \(1\) and \(A = \{a_1, a_2\}\), one has \(\mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow A,\, a_2 \leftrightarrow b) = 1\) but \(\mathbb{P}(o \leftrightarrow b,\, o \leftrightarrow A) = 1/2\). When the minimiser is unique the question is open; it is then equivalent to the version for every minimiser under the restriction \(\mathbb{P}(o \nleftrightarrow A) > 0\), and it suffices to treat edge probabilities in \((0, 1)\).

In the paper, Conjectures 2 and 4 are proved when \(A\) has two vertices (Theorems 1 and 7), when \(A\) has three vertices under extra hypotheses (Theorems 2, 3 and 11), when \(o\) is isolated in \(G \setminus A\) or joined only to \(A\) and to one further vertex (Theorems 4 and 5), and for the functions \(f = \mathbf{1}\{|C| \ge k\}\) with \(k \le 4\) on every graph (Theorem 9).

The method

The proofs run the development's argument with two changes. First, the clusters of the vertices of \(A\) are conditioned on missing a given set \(Y\) of vertices: if the vertices of \(A\) are ordered so that \(\mathbb{E}[F(C_x) \mid x \nleftrightarrow Y]\) is nondecreasing, then \[ \sum_{x \in A} \mathbb{P}\bigl(o \nleftrightarrow Y,\; o \leftrightarrow x,\; o \nleftrightarrow y \text{ for every } y \in A \text{ before } x\bigr)\, \mathbb{E}[F(C_x) \mid x \nleftrightarrow Y] \;\le\; \mathbb{E}[F(C_o);\; o \nleftrightarrow Y,\; o \leftrightarrow A]. \] Second, \(F(K)\) is replaced by \(F(K)\) minus the expectation of \(F\) at the cluster of \(a\) in the graph with every edge at a vertex of \(K\) removed; this new function is increasing in \(K\). Taking \(Y = \{a\}\) for the vertex \(a\) of least mean makes every conditional expectation on the left nonnegative, and the inequality becomes Conjecture 4 with the minimiser specified. The other conjectures and questions follow from that statement. For Conjecture 6 and Question 9 the cluster of \(o\) is replaced by the union of the clusters of a set of vertices: the union of the clusters of \(v\) and \(w\) does not depend on whether the edge \(\{v, w\}\) is open, and the cluster of \(o\) is the union of the clusters of \(o\) and of its open neighbours in the graph with the edges at \(o\) removed.

Implications

  • The paper's chain of implications, Conjecture 4 to (3) to Conjecture 2 to Conjecture 1 to Conjecture 3 to \(\theta(p_c) = 0\), holds with every link proved, the last one in the development.
  • The vertex of \(A\) minimising \(\mathbb{P}(x \leftrightarrow b)\) is itself a valid choice in the pre-FKG comparison (Question 7), for every graph and every size of \(A\); so is the minimiser computed in the graph with the edges at \(o\) removed (Question 9).
  • Conjecture 6 holds without its hypothesis (39), so the paper's Lemma 13 applies without it.
  • The conditioned inequality of the method holds for the union of the clusters of any finite set of vertices in place of \(C_o\), and it needs no sign condition on \(F\).
