Kozma–Nitzan's Conjectures 2 and 4, formalized on top of the Lean proof of θ(pc) = 0

The Lean 4 / Mathlib development θ(pc) = 0 for Bernoulli bond percolation on ℤd in all dimensions d ≥ 2 (written by Claude, Anthropic, under the direction of Justin Leder; Lean v4.32.0, Mathlib commit 81a5d257) proves Conjecture 3 of Kozma–Nitzan (arXiv:2401.12397) through a family of conditioned correlation inequalities, the conditioned slack hierarchy. The Lean files in conjectures-1-4/ prove, on top of that development, the paper's Conjectures 2 and 4 and answer its Question 7. Lean accepts every theorem with exactly the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound, and no sorry (VERIFICATION.log).

What is proved

Bernoulli bond percolation on a finite weighted graph (vertices Fin n, independent pairs with weights in [0,1], an absent edge being weight 0), a nonempty relay set A, vertices o and b, and an increasing real function F of vertex sets:

  • Conjecture 4 (p. 32), in the stronger fixed-minimiser form: if a ∈ A minimises E F(Cx) over A, then E[F(Ca); o ↔ A] ≤ E[F(Co); o ↔ A]; hence minx∈A E[F(Cx); o ↔ A] ≤ E[F(Co); o ↔ A] (conjecture4Fixed_holds, conjecture4_holds).
  • Conjecture 2 (p. 3), in the strong pre-FKG form (3): minx∈A P(x ↔ b, o ↔ A) ≤ P(o ↔ b, o ↔ A); hence minx∈A P(o ↔ A, x ↔ b) ≤ P(o ↔ b) (conjecture2Strong_holds, conjecture2_holds).
  • Question 7 (p. 36), answered affirmatively: if a ∈ A minimises P(a ↔ b) over A, then P(o ↔ A, a ↔ b) ≤ P(o ↔ b, o ↔ A), the paper's display (41) (question7_holds).

Conjectures 2 and 4 were known only for two relays and for an observer isolated in G ∖ A (the paper's Theorems 1, 4, 7 and 8). The same file also exports Conjecture 1, which is essentially the development's own result, and the development's Conjecture 3.

The idea

The development proves a first-relay bound: ordering the relays by E F(Cx), Σx∈A P(x is o's first relay)·E F(Cx) ≤ E[F(Co); o ↔ A]. The new ingredient is an avoided version of it, in which the observer's cluster is required to avoid a set Y and the relay means are conditioned on x ↮ Y; the development's hierarchy already carries an avoided set, so its peeling argument goes through with Y inserted into every avoidance set. With Y = {a}, a the relay of least mean, and the projected functional φa(K) = F(K) − E[F(Ca) | Co = K], which is increasing in K because a larger explored cluster leaves a smaller cluster for a, every conditional relay mean equals (E F(Cx) − E F(Ca)) / P(x ↮ a) ≥ 0, so E[(F(Co) − F(Ca)); o ↮ a, o ↔ A] ≥ 0; on {o ↔ a} the two clusters coincide. That is Conjecture 4 with the fixed minimiser; F = 1{b ∈ ·} gives (3) and Question 7. The route is the one proposed in the ChatGPT document.

What it implies

  • The paper's chain 4 ⟹ (3) ⟹ 2 ⟹ 1 ⟹ 3 ⟹ θ(pc) = 0 is now formal from top to bottom.
  • The relay minimising the unconditional mean is itself a valid relay in the pre-FKG comparison (Question 7); the answer is uniform in the graph and in the number of relays.
  • The avoided first-relay bound (genY_all) is a general conditioned gluing inequality with no loss in the number of relays, available for further use.

Nothing is claimed about Conjecture 6 or Questions 5, 8 and 9.
