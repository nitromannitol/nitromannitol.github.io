Kozma–Nitzan's conjectures and questions: what is machine-checked and what is not

The Lean 4 / Mathlib development θ(pc) = 0 for Bernoulli bond percolation on ℤd in all dimensions d ≥ 2 (written by Claude, Anthropic, under the direction of Justin Leder; Lean v4.32.0, Mathlib commit 81a5d257) proves Conjecture 3 of Kozma–Nitzan (arXiv:2401.12397) through a family of conditioned correlation inequalities, the conditioned slack hierarchy. The paper has nine numbered open items: Conjectures 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 and Questions 5, 7, 8 and 9. The document kozma_nitzan_all_conjectures_proof (TeX) proposes proofs of all nine on top of the development's methods. The Lean files in lean/ machine-check the part concerning Conjectures 1, 2, 3 and 4 and Questions 5 and 7; Lean accepts every theorem with exactly the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound, and no sorry (VERIFICATION.log). Conjecture 6 and Question 9 are proved in the document only; Question 8 is not settled. The status of each item is stated below.

Machine-checked

Bernoulli bond percolation on a finite weighted graph (vertices Fin n, independent pairs with weights in [0,1], an absent edge being weight 0), a nonempty relay set A, vertices o and b, and an increasing real function F of vertex sets:

  • Conjecture 4 (p. 32), in the stronger fixed-minimiser form: if a ∈ A minimises E F(Cx) over A, then E[F(Ca); o ↔ A] ≤ E[F(Co); o ↔ A]; hence minx∈A E[F(Cx); o ↔ A] ≤ E[F(Co); o ↔ A] (conjecture4Fixed_holds, conjecture4_holds); also in the paper's vocabulary of monotone cluster properties f(v, ω) (conjecture4_clusterProperty_holds).
  • Conjecture 2 (p. 3), in the strong pre-FKG form (3): minx∈A P(x ↔ b, o ↔ A) ≤ P(o ↔ b, o ↔ A); hence minx∈A P(o ↔ A, x ↔ b) ≤ P(o ↔ b) (conjecture2Strong_holds, conjecture2_holds).
  • Question 7 (p. 36), answered affirmatively: if a ∈ A minimises P(a ↔ b) over A, then P(o ↔ A, a ↔ b) ≤ P(o ↔ b, o ↔ A), the paper's display (41) (question7_holds).
  • Conjecture 1 (p. 3): P(o ↔ A) · minx∈A P(x ↔ b) ≤ P(o ↔ b) (conjecture1_holds, from (3) by the Harris inequality; it is also a short corollary of the development's own first-relay bound).
  • Conjecture 3 (p. 15), from Conjecture 1 (conjecture3_holds).
  • Question 5 (p. 32), answered affirmatively: there are coefficients ca ≥ 0, a ∈ A, summing to 1 and not depending on b, with Σa∈A ca P(o ↔ A, a ↔ b) ≤ P(o ↔ b) for every b, the paper's display (38) (question5_holds; from Conjecture 4 for weighted vertex counts, question5_dual, and finite-dimensional separation).
  • Conjecture 6 (p. 34), reductions: its strong form implies the paper's display (40) (conjecture6_of_conjecture6Strong); its hypothesis (39) is a consequence of Conjecture 1 (conjecture6_hypotheses_vacuous); the strong form holds whenever the minimiser is taken in the graph with the edge forced open (conjecture6Strong_of_forceOpen_min); and the pair-source projection identities and the source–relay overlap lemma of the document (PairSource). Conjecture 6 itself is not machine-checked.

In print, Conjectures 2 and 4 are proved for two relays (Theorems 1 and 7), for three relays under extra hypotheses (Theorems 2, 3 and 11), for an observer isolated in G ∖ A and its one-vertex extension (Theorems 4 and 5), and, for every graph and every relay set, for the cluster properties f = 1{|C| ≥ k} with k ≤ 4 (Theorem 9). Both were open in general. The strong form of Conjecture 6 used here is the document's Theorem 6.2 (a fixed minimiser, no endpoint hypotheses).

Proved in the document, not machine-checked

  • Conjecture 6, in the strong form: for e = {v, w} and a ∈ A minimising PG(x ↔ b) over A, PG⏚e(v ↔ b, v ↔ A) ≥ PG⏚e(a ↔ b, v ↔ A), where G⏚e has the edge e forced open; display (40) follows, without the paper's hypothesis (39). The document's first version of this argument contained an error (an exact conditional identity for a two-vertex source that fails; a four-vertex counterexample was found by a Codex audit). The corrected argument explores the whole source cluster and carries a nonnegative correction term. It was audited by two further Codex passes and one Claude Opus pass: no gap and no counterexample, with exact rational enumeration of every inequality in the chain on graphs with four to six vertices (several thousand instances, no violation). A Lean formalization of this argument is in progress.
  • Question 9 (p. 36): the minimiser of PH(a ↔ b), H the graph with all edges at o removed, satisfies (41). The document's proof conditions on the edges at o and applies the source-set theorem to the random set of open neighbours; it needs that theorem for source sets of every size. Not machine-checked. Exhaustive searches over four-vertex weighted graphs found no counterexample, in either reading of the minimiser.
  • Countable graphs: the document extends the inequalities to countable graphs and re-derives θ(pc) = 0 through the paper's Theorem 6. The development already contains θ(pc) = 0 as a Lean theorem, so this adds no new statement.

Not settled

Question 8 (p. 36) takes the minimiser of P(a ↔ b, o ↮ A) over A, with o not joined to A. The document's Question 8 argument instead uses the minimiser of P(o ↔ A, a ↔ b), for which (41) is the machine-checked strong form of Conjecture 2; that is a different question. For the paper's Question 8, in the reading where (41) must hold for every minimiser, there is an exact four-vertex counterexample on the degenerate locus P(o ↮ A) = 0, where the minimand vanishes identically. In the reading with a unique minimiser no counterexample was found in exhaustive four-vertex grid searches, and no proof is known. A separate note, Q8 (TeX), states the printed question, gives the four-vertex counterexample, proves that when P(o ↮ A) > 0 the unique-minimiser and every-minimiser versions are equivalent, reduces the remaining problem to interior edge weights and a strict minimiser, and gives a sufficient condition; its conclusion is the same: the strict reading is unproved.

Implications

  • The paper's chain 4 ⟹ (3) ⟹ 2 ⟹ 1 ⟹ 3 ⟹ θ(pc) = 0 is formal from top to bottom: the first four implications in Conjectures.lean, the last in the development (its formalization of the paper's Theorem 6).
  • Of the paper's nine numbered items, six are machine-checked (Conjectures 1–4, Questions 5 and 7), two have written proofs in the document that passed independent audits but are not yet in Lean (Conjecture 6, Question 9), and one remains open (Question 8).
  • The relay minimising the unconditional connection probability is itself a valid relay in the pre-FKG comparison (Question 7), uniformly in the graph and in the number of relays.
  • Conjecture 6 holds without either of its endpoint hypotheses (document); hypothesis (39) is in any case a consequence of Conjecture 1 (Lean).
  • A source-set version of the avoided first-relay bound, for sources of every size, would settle Question 9 by the document's argument; the pair-source case gives Conjecture 6.
