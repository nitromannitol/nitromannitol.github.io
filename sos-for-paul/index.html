Current scaling-limit predictions

Rough regime · favored

Non-Gaussian, H = (α−2)/q

At (q, α) = (0.5, 2.125), (1, 2.25), (1.5, 2.375), predict a non-Gaussian limit of β1/qn−5/4Σfφ. The n = 512 kurtoses are 0.531, 0.340, 0.113. The exponent is less secure at q = 1.5 and closer to the proposed transition.

α = 3.5 · favored

Gaussian Brownian bridge

For q = 0.5, 1, 1.5, predict β1/qn−3/2Σfφ → σ⟨BD, f⟩, with deterministic σ > 0. Fitted H = 0.496, 0.496, 0.492; kurtosis and covariance ratios are compatible with the bridge.

α = 1.75 · tentative

Fractional Gaussian distribution

Predict β1/qn−7/8Σfφ → σ⟨G1.75, f⟩. The trend favors Gaussian smooth averages, but powers and covariance ratios still drift. This is a weaker prediction.

At q = 2, Gaussianity is exact. Along α = 2 + q/4, our working prediction is that the non-Gaussian family approaches the quadratic Gaussian limit as q ↑ 2.

At α = 2 and near α = 2 + q/2, no limiting law has been selected by these data. The original Gaussian critical limits, logarithms, and transition location remain provisional. At q = 0.5, α = 2.4, κ(f) is still 0.323 at n = 512, despite being above the proposed boundary.

The full non-Gaussian law is still unidentified; the Brownian-cutoff construction is an untested candidate. BD is the Brownian bridge on (−1,1); Gα uses the restricted fractional operator with zero exterior. See the precise current statement and its qualifications.
