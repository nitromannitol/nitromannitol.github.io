Predict β1/qn−7/8Σfφ → σ⟨G1.75, f⟩. The trend favors Gaussian smooth averages, but powers and covariance ratios still drift. This is a weaker prediction.

At q = 2, Gaussianity is exact. Along α = 2 + q/4, our working prediction is that the non-Gaussian family approaches the quadratic Gaussian limit as q ↑ 2.

At α = 2 and near α = 2 + q/2, no limiting law has been selected by these data. The original Gaussian critical limits, logarithms, and transition location remain provisional. At q = 0.5, α = 2.4, κ(f) is still 0.323 at n = 512, despite being above the proposed boundary.

The full non-Gaussian law is still unidentified; the Brownian-cutoff construction is an untested candidate. BD is the Brownian bridge on (−1,1); Gα uses the restricted fractional operator with zero exterior. See the precise current statement and its qualifications.

The displayed exponents and critical logarithms are the original hypotheses. Intervals measure Monte Carlo uncertainty; finite-size bias remains. All intervals are pointwise, so scanning many parameter pairs increases the chance of a false signal.

What was measured

The even test function is f(t) = exp(1 − 1/(1 − (t/0.8)²)) on |t| < 0.8 and zero elsewhere. The odd function is g(t) = 3t f(t)/0.8. We also record a narrow central bump, two separated bumps, point heights, increments and energy.

The mixed fourth residual is [E(X²Y²) − E(X²)E(Y²) − 2E(XY)²]/[Var(X)Var(Y)], with centered X and Y. It vanishes for jointly Gaussian observables. We compare several projections because Gaussian-looking marginals alone do not identify a field law.

R̂ below 1.01 and at least 1,000 estimated effective samples in all three displayed smooth projections are screening criteria. Passing these checks does not prove equilibration. The exact energy mean and variance, each divided by (2n+1)/q, should equal one.
