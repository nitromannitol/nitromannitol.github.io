An untested candidate for the full non-Gaussian law

Fix 2 < α < 2 + q/2 and H = (α−2)/q. Let B be a standard Brownian bridge on D, extended by zero outside. For any fixed η > 0 and cutoff ε > 0, set Uε = η εH−1/2B. Define a probability law νε by weighting this Gaussian reference:

Eε(u) = ∬|x−y|>ε |u(x)−u(y)|q / |x−y|α dx dy
νε(du) = Zε−1 exp[−Eε(u)] Law(Uε)(du).

The integral is over ordered pairs in ℝ², including the zero exterior. For each positive cutoff this is a specified probability measure. My stronger conjecture is that νε converges as ε ↓ 0, independently of η, and that its limit Q(q,α) is the lattice scaling limit. It should be non-Gaussian for q < 2, reduce to Gα for q = 2, and scale with exponent H when the domain is dilated.

Equivalently, this proposes the complete characteristic functional, including joint laws:

E exp(i⟨Q(q,α),f⟩) = limε↓0 E[exp(i⟨Uε,f⟩ − Eε(Uε))] / E[exp(−Eε(Uε))].

Existence, independence of η and agreement with the lattice are all conjectural. The current simulations have not tested this cutoff construction. The cutoff is essential: an unregularized expression exp(−E(u)) Du is not a definition of a probability law. “Random covariance” or a generalized-normal marginal alone would not specify this joint law.

Gaussian line, constants, and falsifiable predictions

Gα is the centered Gaussian field with covariance (4Lα)−1, where Lαu(x) = PV ∫ℝ(u(x)−u(y))/|x−y|α dy and u = 0 outside D. This is the restricted, zero-exterior operator. At q = 2, the limit is exactly Gα for 1 < α < 3, ½B at α = 3 under aₙ = √(n/log n), and [4ζ(α−2)]−1/2B at α > 3 under aₙ = √n.

The original nonlinear conjecture predicts nonzero limiting fourth cumulants in the rough interval and vanishing normalized fourth cumulants at and above the upper crossover. At q = 1, α = 2.5, it proposes √n/log n. The new sweep tests this point: the fitted effective log power is −0.695, compared with the proposed −1, and κ(f) = 0.209 [0.120, 0.305] at n = 512. The limiting logarithm and Gaussianity remain unresolved.

For the measured point, n−5/4Σfφ is the proposed pairing. At n = 1024 the even-minus-odd kurtosis interval is [0.052, 0.377], arguing against one independent scalar amplitude multiplying a Gaussian field. At n = 2048 that difference is less precise and its interval includes zero. Neither result singles out the stronger cutoff candidate.

How to interpret this experiment

For q = 1, α = 2.25, the candidate amplitude exponent is H = ¼. The corresponding smooth pairing includes the factor 1/n from a Riemann sum, giving n−5/4Σf(x/n)φ(x). Your n−1/2 normalization is also evaluated on exactly the same configurations.

We examine the even bump, an odd bump, and several linear combinations. Gaussian-looking individual marginals alone cannot establish joint Gaussianity. Excess kurtosis, Q–Q plots and a mixed fourth cumulant probe different aspects; no normality-test p-values assuming independent MCMC draws are used.

Three chains start from different profiles. Critical logarithms and the β1/q amplitude factor are included in new candidate-normalized runs. Split R̂ is computed for the raw and squared even-bump observables; autocorrelation estimates and block resampling address dependent samples. Passing these checks does not guarantee equilibration. Increase the budget and n to test persistence.

A fractional Brownian motion claim additionally needs the appropriate covariance, self-similarity and tightness. On this fixed Dirichlet domain the limit would have boundary effects; it should not be compared directly with unconditioned fBm pinned at the origin.
