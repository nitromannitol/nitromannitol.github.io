Reading notes: long-range Gaussian and q-SOS chains

Source: Coquille, Dario and Le Ny, arXiv:2412.15782v2, downloaded September 8, 2026. The downloaded PDF has 45 pages; its title page is dated July 14, 2026. The full extracted text is 2412.15782.txt.

Main results and proof structure

The observable is the variance of the centre height as N increases, at fixed parameters. For q = 2, its growth is bounded for 1 < α < 2, N^(α−2) for 2 < α < 3, N/log N at α = 3, and N for α > 3. At α = 2, sufficiently high temperature gives logarithmic growth and sufficiently low temperature gives localisation; no exact critical β is specified.

For 0 < q < 2, the variance lower bounds are constant for α ≤ 2, N1 for 2 < α < 2+q/2, N/(log N)^(2/q) at equality, and N above it. Upper bounds are constant for α < q, logarithmic at α = q, and N^min(2α/q−2,1) above. The linear cap is supplied at the end of §4.2.2. Bounds need not match.

The proofs use variance monotonicity under graph surgery. Lower bounds reduce to chains; upper bounds combine paths. For q-SOS, Gaussian mixtures with random conductances and FKG comparisons extend the argument.

Numerical interpretation used here

The code implements the displayed finite-volume definitions literally. Its active sites are −N through N, inclusive: 2N + 1 active sites, with the entire exterior fixed to zero. The graph representation in Remark 2.3 uses a shifted endpoint convention. Do not combine that site’s indexing with the displayed definitions without translating N.

For an unordered internal pair, its contribution to the Hamiltonian is

2 |h_i − h_j|^q / |i − j|^α.

For a single active site i, the exterior contribution is

2 b_i |h_i|^q,
b_i = ζ(α, N+i+1) + ζ(α, N−i+1).

Here ζ(s,a) means the sum of r^(−s) over integers r ≥ a. These two tails account for every frozen exterior site. The factor 2 follows the ordered sum over i,j in Definitions 1.1/1.4 (excluding i=j). If using an unordered-edge convention without that factor, multiply the inverse temperature by 2 to reproduce these simulations.

The implementation evaluates each tail with 32 explicit terms and five Euler–Maclaurin corrections. This is a floating point evaluation of an infinite sum, not an exact symbolic evaluation. It avoids an interaction-distance cutoff and subtraction of nearly equal zeta sums.

Sampling and interpretation

Every sweep attempts one random single-site update per active site on average, followed by four interval updates. Each proposal translates the selected site or interval by an integer. Interval lengths are sampled over powers of two, including the whole chain. The sign and magnitude distributions are symmetric and independent of the heights, so forward and reverse proposal probabilities match. Accepting with min(1, exp(−β ΔH)) gives the Gibbs measure as an invariant distribution. Unit moves make the integer state space accessible. This is an equilibrium sampler; its sweep counter is not physical time.

For an interval translation, pairs inside the interval have unchanged height differences. The energy difference sums only pairs crossing the interval boundary and interactions with the frozen exterior. No height truncation is imposed.

The interface is started flat. Samples are discarded during the selected warmup and recorded at the chosen interval thereafter. Sign symmetry gives an exact equilibrium centre mean of zero, so the displayed variance estimate is the mean of h_0². The observed centre mean is also shown. RMS height averages squared heights over sites and samples; it is a different observable.

Batch estimates use groups of 64 recorded squared heights. Estimated errors and effective sample counts can miss correlations longer than a batch. A flat-looking cold chain, a short run, or a poor symmetry check is not evidence of localisation. In particular, very small q or β may require much longer runs and larger proposal scales to explore typical configurations.

The optional size experiment runs three seeds at each of N = 8,16,32,64, recording 2,000 samples per seed after at least 2,000 warmup sweeps. Error bars use the larger of the standard error between seed estimates and the combined batch errors. These are exploratory measurements, not confidence guarantees or numerical proofs of the asymptotic statements.

For q = 2, define the positive matrix L with off-diagonal entries −|i−j|^(−α) and diagonal entries 2ζ(α). Then H(h) = 2hᵀLh and the continuous Gaussian covariance is (4βL)⁻¹. A Cholesky solve supplies the dashed reference in the size plot. The discrete sampler is not generated by rounding continuous Gaussian samples.

Reproducibility and checks

node test-model.cjs checks tails, all local and interval energy differences on small interfaces, inverse moves, energy drift, normalization, seeded repeatability and the Gaussian covariance. It also compares Monte Carlo estimates with a separately implemented three-site exact enumeration for q = 0.75, 1, 2, checking two height windows for enumeration stability.

CSV exports contain the seed, parameters, warmup, thinning, sweep index, centre height, its square, energy per site and spatial mean square height. At most the latest 50,000 recorded configurations are retained for export; displayed running statistics use all post-warmup samples. The JSON export from the size experiment contains each seed’s estimates and sampling settings.
